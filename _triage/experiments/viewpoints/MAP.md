## Language selection after two rounds

We compared **fourteen viewpoints**, tested the initial shortlist, and expanded
the search when both shortlisted approaches reproduced existing obligations.
The preferred working language for the next bounded investigation is
**V13: arithmetic regularity of orbit-component colorings**. Its question is:

> If some integers never reach 1, how complicated must the arithmetic
> description separating their components from the component of 1 be?

This shifts attention to the representation of an obstruction. It already
has a checkable consequence: every eventually periodic invariant coloring
for `3x+1` is constant. For `3x+5` and `3x+61`, explicit nonconstant periodic
colorings survive. The [complete elementary argument](V002/invariant_colors.md)
identifies the exact role of the additive parameter. It is independently
reviewed here, with no novelty or formal-verification claim.

The intermediate target of classifying invariant colorings recognized
by a finite automaton in **base 2 alone** is now proved in
[V004](V004/GLOBAL_RIGIDITY.md). Positive-offset
binary sections also form a finite ternary-closed family. Cobham and an
elementary residue argument force constancy. We have no reason to assume
an actual nonabsorbing set has that regularity. This demonstrated gain
does not establish a superior convergence method.

The complementary **V14: binary/ternary carry diagram** test is now complete:
it adds no new section relation. Retain **V12: laws of actual integer
ensembles** as a distinct alternative; it has not supplied a new first-passage
estimate.

The first round's information-loss lesson remains: simplifying a representation
can discard exactly what is needed to recover an actual positive-integer path.
Guarded affine histories preserve that information well, but the proposed
relational extension was already present in the frozen programme. Graph
geometry controlled local boundary errors without giving an average estimate.

The frozen results remain useful: they identify which quantities cannot serve
as progress measures and provide exact difficult examples. We preserve them
while changing the questions asked of them.

- **Partial classification:** every invariant `h(n)=F(s_2(n))` is constant,

## V005: the quantitative target and its first discrimination tests

The [first report](V005/REPORT.md) proves that an
automatic positive binary cylinder would force global constancy, with a
polynomial reconstruction bound at every depth. It also transfers binary
growth to the **joint** binary/ternary section family. The active target

Two exact examples rule out shortcuts: all descendant families can be

## V006: quadratic amplification and the extraction gap

The [report](V006/REPORT.md) proves pairwise

and no digit-count section while satisfying the reduced binary identities.
It fails full Collatz invariance. The new theorem leaves extraction for
arbitrary invariants unresolved.

The semigroup audit rejects a direct import from transformation growth:

whether the full constraints permit them is the next bounded test. The

## V007: singleton separators excluded at every depth

The [report](V007/REPORT.md) proves that a finite
forward interior cannot have a single exit in the full joint section
graph of a nonconstant invariant. Reverse closure first supplies infinite
ternary kernels at every positive binary section, complementing V005's
binary result. The six radix identities then propagate a putative single
exit and force a repeated whole sequence, a contradiction.

This proves at least two new joint states at every depth. It remains a
linear bound; after the depth conversion it is weaker than the generic
binary bound. The gain is exclusion of a possible graph structure without
the V006 digit-count hypothesis. The next bounded test keeps distinct
destinations in a two-exit boundary. The full target requires every
possible linear coefficient; singleton or two-exit results alone cannot
complete it. The parallel literature audit supplies no applicable general
growth theorem under the present hypotheses.

## V008: the two-exit test changes the required mechanism

The [report](V008/REPORT.md) gives an actual sequence
with two exits and infinite kernels in both bases at every vertex. It
obeys all radix laws, fails a full Collatz root identity and has quadratic
joint growth. This refutes a generic exclusion of every finite two-exit
interior, while leaving the linear-growth question intact.

The three full scalar identities do force constancy if the initial joint
ball has at most three states. The exhaustive corrected argument is
elementary, with no Cobham dependency, and gives no asymptotic gain.
Separately, linear cumulative growth supplies arbitrarily late cuts whose
entire future has a uniform cumulative mean bound. It does not supply
long intervals of bounded layer width; an explicit bounded-degree graph
control disproves that counting inference.

Use the genuine cumulative bound and full root information in subsequent
tests. Do not proceed through fixed boundary cardinalities as if the
singleton theorem automatically generalized. Near-minimal binary growth
has a precise eventual single-vertex-tail formulation that can test this
mechanism, but the full target remains every possible linear coefficient.

## V009: a universal additive gain and a global detection theorem

The [report](V009/REPORT.md) proves that an eventual
single-state binary tail is impossible. Its maximal transitions reduce
to periodic phases; a rational-coordinate argument and exact radix swaps
then make every return block contract in depth. Including the finite
earlier states gives bounded return families and equality of whole
sections, contradicting their distinct minimum depths. Therefore every
nonconstant invariant satisfies `kappa_h(D)-(D+1)->infinity`.

This is a universal additive improvement. It permits D+sqrt(D), so it
does not give a fixed fractional improvement or the required unbounded
ratio. Extending the contraction to multiple states per layer remains
open; cumulative growth does not supply a uniform layer-width bound.

A separate elementary theorem directly uses the global hypothesis
`|J_D|<=C(D+1)`: every nonzero linear combination of depth-A joint
sections must be detected using O_C(sqrt(A+1)) input digits. A sufficiently
late first nonzero value produces quadratically many independent mixed
sections. The exact cutoff and a sufficient delayed-combination criterion
are written; constructing those combinations from arbitrary invariance
is still missing. Compare that route with extension of return contraction.

## Three discrimination probes

### 1. Which simplifications preserve legal paths?

The [lifting probe](V001/lifting_probe.md) compares V2
and V3. For the two odd arrows with valuations `(1,4)`, the exact map is
`(9x+5)/32` on `x=3 mod64`. Keeping only its slope loses the offset; reversing
the word changes the offset; using modulus32 wrongly admits35, whose actual
word is `(1,5)`. An integral rational-factor step can also be illegal:
`10*(3/5)=6`, but the shortcut image of6 is3, not10.

The inspected published semigroup step first multiplies `x=7 mod64` by5,
then reaches `(45x+5)/64<x` in six shortcut arrows. It is a valid step in
that relaxed problem. A Collatz comparison between x and5x is still missing.
**Decision:** keep guarded composition; defer semigroup lifting without a
new way to supply the bridge. No novelty is claimed for either calculation.

### 2. Does a finite exit counter supply progress?

The [summary probe](V001/summary_probe.md) tests V8/V9
on the frozen R31 growth pattern. An unbounded valuation counter decreases
by11 per guarded block, while the affine height grows. Its exact exhaustion
therefore proves exit from one specified guard. It does not prove descent,
and leaving that guard need not end the raw valuation word.

This reproduces structure already present in R31/R77; later frozen work
already retains asynchronous source histories. **Decision:** no new unary
return catalogue. A relational pilot needs an explicit invariant across
different histories or phases that simplifies an existing obligation.

### 3. Does a geometric potential add a usable bound?

The [potential probe](V001/potential_probe.md) uses
conductance1/n on actual odd arrows. On the finite domain `{1,3,5}`, the
voltage at3 and5 is less than1 because all omitted inverse edges lead to
the escape boundary. Deleting those edges gives a false finite-domain
value. On the d=5 nonabsorbing cycle, voltage is0.

Positive voltage certifies a finite path into the component of1, already
recognized by the current framework. **Decision:** the only possible new
benefit is a quantitative estimate across growing domains. Recurrence,
connectivity restatements or a favorable finite plot are insufficient.

## Decision rules

- A successful language must improve a checkable proof obligation or expose
  useful structure, not just carry the same missing premise under another name.
- Finite calculations test the translation. They do not establish uniform
  bounds, termination, integer realization of infinite words or novelty.
- Keep both real size and arithmetic legality when needed; preserve signs,
  source weights, exact endpoints, absorber conventions and all clocks.
- A claim must behave correctly on the d=5 and d=61 cycle controls. The
  failure of a general argument there must occur at a named hypothesis.
- Fixed-word periodic exclusions are partial cycle results. They say
  nothing by themselves about aperiodic divergence.
- A recommendation is provisional and revisable. Its evidence must distinguish
  useful organization from a claimed advance on convergence. No long proof
  campaign follows automatically from selecting a working language.
