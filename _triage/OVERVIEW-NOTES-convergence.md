## R1–R4 and the PR1 controls (from B07)

The convergence programme began from a ChatGPT hand-off (`packet-currents-snapshot`, R1). It turns the stopping-work discrepancy of a signed source measure into "packets" of exact source lifts whose weights sum to zero. A finite signed current J with (I − P)J = μ then bounds the packets' work at every future horizon, keeping cancellations between orbits that meet at different times (`finite-current-certificate-identity`). R1 certified this at two named cutoffs but could not make the cost uniform (`uniform-current-cost`). The main lane then grouped depth-one lifts from the right. That leaves the remainder on the fixed core {5, 7, 11, 13}, with an O(log N) error at every horizon (`right-aligned-fixed-core-remainder`, R2), and an exact error in [−12, 1] at dyadic cutoffs (`dyadic-cutoff-core-remainder`, R4). A maximum principle with the comparison function N^(3/2) proves that finite currents, of any cost and with nonpositive residual, at every cutoff are equivalent to Collatz (`qualitative-current-criterion`, R3). This makes certificate supply the frontier (`packet-current-supply`), but it is a reformulation, not a weakening. An independent lane (PR1) re-derived R2 and R3 and found no gap. It also showed that 3x+61 matches Collatz on every cutoff up to 212 and fails at 213, so generic arguments cannot work (`locally-collatz-like-control-barrier`). It showed that progress statistics do not separate 3x+1 from 3x+61 (`progress-measures-control-3x-61`), and that the obligation splits class by class into "no cycles" and "no divergence" (`class-wise-obligation-equivalence`). Its minimality lemma survives only with its no-cycle hypothesis: cheapest one-sided certificates are not unique (`exact-current-minimality`).

## R5–R10 (from B08)

R5 cuts the cutoffs needed in the current criterion down to five dyadic rays. It uses a ray-dependent comparison weight with contraction 89/90 and a finite base below 96, and the PR1 lane re-read the proof. R6 shows that multiplying a cutoff by 64 adds a disjoint six-band annulus A_N, so the target becomes currents with nonpositive residual for every A_N on 30 sequences, whose starting cutoffs are already settled. R6 constructs no current, because its source-scale quotient keeps an expanding 3N/2 term. R7 says exactly what currents detect: the total mass of each orbit component. It gives a complete but non-terminating search, and shows that bounded per-packet catalogues cannot work. R8 builds an explicit partial current for every annulus, removing positive mass 73N/48 + O(1) out of 99N/4, and shows positive current cost cannot be avoided (H₅₃(A_96)=699). R9 compresses each annulus to 19/22 of its norm in one pass, but the pass cannot be repeated. Literal forward transport also needs ever finer ternary moduli. R10 reduces construction to a finite integer potential Φ on exposed graphs and names the missing term of the scale argument, an explicit boundary flux. It also proves that coarse residue views and scalar work lose the needed information. The missing piece, a lemma that every positive Φ-plateau ends, is the open question that the residual-progress line (R11–R94) then pursued.

## R11–R24 (from B09)

R11 makes the five-ray implication finite and constructive: a rational cone with contraction r = 35/27 and a strict integer gap (positive residual below one unit rounds away on a finite graph) compiles supplied one-sided packet certificates into an exact integer absorption current, demonstrated for U_112 from thirty actual inputs; but the inputs may lie above the target, so the compiler cannot feed itself (`finite-rational-cone-integer-certificate`, `actual-graph-current-compiler`). R14 shows that the automatic input from inner absorption is circular and returns the unresolved complement unchanged (`local-packet-calibration-extension`). A run of exact obstructions then rules out every bounded strategy: fixed words in root collapse and transport (R12, `root-transport-normal-form`), any fixed exposure depth, proved by an inverse-history invariant after a complete depth-32 witness and its infinite lift (R13, `unbounded-exposure-depth-necessary`, which answers `uniform-aggregate-exposure-depth`), the short delay 2h+1 in most ternary phases (R19) and any depth bound depending only on the ternary order, even at h = 2 (R22, `fixed-ternary-order-unbounded-depth`). Positive partial results grew alongside: explicit zero-cost cancellation after arbitrarily long isolation (R16), packing that removes 1073N/432 + O(1) of the 99N/4 positive annular mass (R16-b), terminating normalizations whose positive remainders are exact and unavoidable (R15, R18), and a sharp 63-arrow span for annular sources on one restricted path, whose exits can lead into source-empty components (R21). From R17 the line pivoted to the outer complement of a single cutoff: given absorption through N, the retained cutoff graph leaves an explicit profile R_N of mass 25N/288 + O(1), exactly the shell climbers outside S([1,N]) ∪ S²([1,N]) (`outer-residual-strong-induction-support`, found by the side lane and proved by the main lane). Annular transfer (R20) and the halving identity (R23) certify parts of R_N, and R24 shows that completing the complementary profile at every interleaved five-ray cutoff would prove Collatz by induction, so the open target `five-ray-complementary-profile-completion` is a sharper form of the convergence problem, not a weakening. The side lane's cylinder experiments show that fixed-bit 2-adic certificates always leave a linear remainder (proved), and suggest heuristically that merge-type certificates only change constants (`cylinder-certificates-outer-residual`).
