\begin{abstract}
At depth one, grouping exact source lifts from the right moves every ungrouped
endpoint into the fixed set $\{5,7,11,13\}$. This gives an error bound valid at
every time horizon. A maximum principle then proves that finite current
certificates for these revised packets at every integer cutoff imply Collatz,
with no uniform bound on certificate size or positive cost. More generally,
finite currents with everywhere nonpositive residual suffice. This is an
exact reformulation of the remaining problem. A further sampling argument
reduces the universal target to five explicit dyadic rays. A disjoint annular
recursion describes the added measure when a cutoff is multiplied by 64.
We also characterize finite-current existence by orbit components and prove
that uniformly bounded certificates for individual packets cannot suffice.
Explicit arithmetic families now supply partial currents for every annulus.
A separate coalescence current reduces its norm to at most $19/22$ of
its original value in one pass. Neither construction has a proved closure
rule for its residual.
A finite rational cone also converts supplied packet certificates and their
boundary terms into integer absorption certificates, using a strict gap
below one unit of positive residual mass.
An inverse-history argument now proves that no fixed historical exposure
depth can certify every actual annulus, even on the single ladder
$N=96\cdot64^k$. Termination with growing depth remains open.
The obstruction persists at every prescribed depth even with the initial
ternary order fixed at two. In a constructive direction, an exact
half-size coalescence identity removes half the leading outer residual.
Completing its complementary family on the interleaved rays would supply
a decreasing-input induction; this completion is not yet proved.
A complete normal form now classifies the elementary bridge identities.
Processing and normalizing a full complementary run pair certifies a further
$55.28\%$ of that residual's leading mass. The remaining return heights
are unbounded. The same normal form supplies infinitely many known targets
with a height-uniform counting theorem; density alone does not prove that
every remaining orbit meets them.
A shrinking signed-pair recursion now resolves explicit infinite families
of those growing returns, with arbitrarily deep recursion. Its positive
integer parameter decreases by more than a factor of four, but some
arithmetic branches remain unresolved. Conversely, repeated bounded run
patterns rule out strict height-monotone progress with any finite label set.
No complete universal current construction, Collatz proof, or novelty claim
is supplied.
\end{abstract}

\section{Definitions and the change in grouping}
Let $S(x)=(3x+1)/2^{v_2(3x+1)}$ on positive odd integers, with $S(1)=1$.
Let $\tau(x)$ be the first hitting time of $1$, possibly infinite, and
$h_L(x)=\min(L,\tau(x))$. Measures below are finite signed integer measures on
odd states greater than $1$. Pushforward $P$ discards mass arriving at $1$.
Write $H_L(\mu)=\langle\mu,h_L\rangle$.

For an integer $N\ge8$, set
\[
b=\lceil\log_2N\rceil,\qquad
n_0=\left\lfloor\frac{N-1}{4}\right\rfloor,\quad
n_1=\left\lfloor\frac{3N+1}{2}\right\rfloor,\quad
n_2=\left\lfloor\frac{3N+1}{4}\right\rfloor,
\]
and retain the handoff's source measure
\[
\nu_N(x)=\mathbf1_{x\le n_1}(3\mathbf1_{x\equiv2\ (3)}-1)
 +\mathbf1_{x\le n_2}(3\mathbf1_{x\equiv1\ (3)}-1).
\]
Its signed total has absolute value at most $8$. For
$Q_T(u)=\sum_{1\le x\le u,\ x\text{ odd}}h_T(x)$ and the corresponding
residue sums $R_{r,T}$, the discrepancy is
\[
\mathcal E_T(N)=R_{5,T}(n_1)-\tfrac13Q_T(n_1)
 +R_{1,T}(n_2)-\tfrac13Q_T(n_2),\qquad
3\mathcal E_T(N)=H_T(\nu_N).
\]

For a single exact valuation $a$, put $M_a=2^{a+1}$. The source and endpoint
lifts are $x_t=q_a+M_at$ and $y_t=e_a+6t$, where
\[
(q_a,e_a,t_0)=
\begin{cases}
((2^a-1)/3,\ 1,\ 1),&a\text{ even},\\
((5\cdot2^a-1)/3,\ 5,\ 0),&a\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}                                                    \tag{1}
\]
The start $t_0$ excludes source $1$ and endpoints already absorbed at $1$.
In component $i\in\{1,2\}$ retain $t_0\le t\le\ell_{a,i}$, where
$\ell_{a,i}=\lfloor(n_i-q_a)/M_a\rfloor$. Set
\[
s_{a,i}=\max(0,\ell_{a,i}-t_0+1),\qquad r_{a,i}=s_{a,i}\bmod3.
\]
Leave the first $r_{a,i}$ lifts ungrouped and partition all remaining lifts
into consecutive triples. Thus the final triple ends at the upper cutoff.
The packet sum over $1\le a\le b$ and both components is denoted $\PiR_N$.
This is a new grouping, distinct from the handoff's left-aligned packets.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-core remainder]
For every integer $N\ge8$,
\[
P\nu_N=\PiR_N+\eta_N,\qquad
\supp\eta_N\subseteq\{5,7,11,13\},\qquad
\|\eta_N\|_1\le6b.                                           \tag{2}
\]
Every raw packet has the form
$c_0\delta_y+c_1\delta_{y+6}+c_2\delta_{y+12}$ with coefficients a
permutation of $(2,-1,-1)$. In particular, $\langle1,\PiR_N\rangle=0$.
Moreover, for every integer $T\ge1$,
\[
\left|3\mathcal E_T(N)-H_{T-1}(\PiR_N)\right|\le8+30b.       \tag{3}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Formula (1) solves $3x+1=2^ay$ with odd $y$, using its least positive source
representative modulo $2^{a+1}$. As $M_a$ is a unit modulo $3$, three
consecutive sources visit all three residues and their coefficients sum to
zero. At most two left lifts remain in each component. They have endpoints
$5,11$ for odd $a$ and $7,13$ for even $a$, and their absolute coefficients
sum to at most $3$ per component.

There is no unaccounted large-valuation tail: $x\le n_1<2^{b+1}$ and
$a(x)>b$ imply
\[
0<S(x)=\frac{3x+1}{2^{a(x)}}<3.
\]
The endpoint is odd, so it equals $1$ and is discarded by $P$. Summing the
leftovers proves (2). The four core hitting times are
\[
\tau(5)=1,\quad\tau(7)=5,\quad\tau(11)=4,\quad\tau(13)=2.
\]
Hence $|H_L(\eta_N)|\le30b$ for every $L$. Finally,
\[
H_T(\nu_N)=\langle1,\nu_N\rangle+H_{T-1}(P\nu_N)
=\langle1,\nu_N\rangle+H_{T-1}(\PiR_N)+H_{T-1}(\eta_N),
\]
which proves (3).
\end{proof}

\section{The qualitative criterion: no uniform current cost is needed}
For functions on nonnegative integers define
\[
(\Lip f)(N)=f(n_0)+\tfrac13f(n_1)+\tfrac13f(n_2).
\]
\begin{lemma}[A strict comparison function]
For every integer $N\ge16$,
\[
\Lip(N^{3/2})<\frac{505}{512}N^{3/2}.                         \tag{4}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The three ratios are at most $1/4$, $49/32$, and $49/64$, respectively.
Consequently
\[
\frac{\Lip(N^{3/2})}{N^{3/2}}
\le\frac18+\frac{343}{384\sqrt2}+\frac{343}{1536}
<\frac18+\frac{245}{384}+\frac{343}{1536}
=\frac{505}{512}<1,
\]
using $1/\sqrt2<5/7$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Equivalent qualitative targets]
The following four statements are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every positive integer reaches $1$ under Collatz.
\item For every integer $N\ge8$, $\sup_{L\ge0}H_L(\PiR_N)<\infty$.
      The bound may depend arbitrarily on $N$.
\item For every integer $N\ge8$, there is a finite signed current $J_N$
      such that $\PiR_N=(I-P)J_N$.
\item For every integer $N\ge8$, there are finite signed measures $J_N,\zeta_N$
      such that $\PiR_N=(I-P)J_N+\zeta_N$ and $\zeta_N\le0$ pointwise.
\end{enumerate}
Neither a uniform cost bound nor an inverse-basin theorem is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Statement 1 implies 3: a finite packet has finitely many sources, so under
Collatz all are absorbed by some finite time $M$. Then
$J_N=\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}P^j\PiR_N$ is finite and telescopes to $\PiR_N$.
Statement 3 implies 4 by taking $\zeta_N=0$. For a finite current,
\[
H_L((I-P)J)=\langle1,J-P^LJ\rangle
=\sum_yJ(y)\mathbf1_{\tau(y)\le L}\le\|J^+\|_1.
\]
Since $h_L\ge0$, a nonpositive residual contributes at most zero. Thus 4
implies 2 without any bound uniform in $N$.

It remains to prove 2 implies 1. Define the exceptional indicator
$e(x)=\mathbf1_{\tau(x)=\infty}$, with $e(1)=0$, and let
\[
B(u)=\sum_{1\le x\le u,\ x\text{ odd}}e(x),\qquad
B_r(u)=\sum_{1\le x\le u,\ x\equiv r\ (6)}e(x).
\]
The indicator is invariant under $S$. Finite support and (2) give
\[
\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{H_L(\PiR_N)}L
=\langle e,\PiR_N\rangle
=\langle e,P\nu_N\rangle
=\langle e,\nu_N\rangle,                                  \tag{5}
\]
because $e$ vanishes on the fixed core. Statement 2 makes (5) nonpositive.

Partitioning sources by first valuation gives the exact exceptional-count
identity
\[
B(N)=B(n_0)+B_5(n_1)+B_1(n_2)
=\Lip B(N)+\tfrac13\langle e,\nu_N\rangle
\le\Lip B(N).                                               \tag{6}
\]
For completeness, valuation $1$ maps $4j+3$ bijectively to $6j+5$;
valuation $2$ maps $8j+1>1$ to $6j+1\ge7$; and valuation at least $3$
has sources $4z+1$ with $z$ odd and $S(4z+1)=S(z)$. The exceptional small
cases $1$ and $5$ both have $e=0$, so (6) has no additive constant.

The odd seeds $1,3,5,7,9,11,13,15$ all terminate, so $B(N)=0$ for $N<16$.
Also $B(N)\le N$, hence $B(N)/N^{3/2}\to0$. If $B$ were nonzero, this
ratio would attain a positive global maximum $M$ at some integer $N\ge16$.
Using (6) and (4) at that maximizer yields
\[
B(N)\le\Lip B(N)\le M\Lip(N^{3/2})
<M N^{3/2}=B(N),
\]
a contradiction. Thus all odd seeds terminate, and removing initial powers
of two gives Statement 1.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{A second check for exact currents.}
There is a short independent proof of $3\Rightarrow1$. If $r$ is the least
nonterminating odd seed, take $N=\lceil(2r-1)/3\rceil$. Then $n_1$ is $r$
or $r+1$, and $n_2<r$. Therefore
\[
\langle e,\nu_N\rangle=3\mathbf1_{r\equiv2\ (3)}-1\in\{-1,2\}.
\]
The core has zero $e$-pairing, whereas every finite divergence has zero
$e$-pairing by invariance. This is impossible. This short proof uses exact
currents; the maximum principle above is what also permits nonpositive residuals.

\section{An exact constant error on dyadic cutoffs}
For $N=2^b$, $b\ge3$, the core remainder depends only on $b\bmod6$:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrr}
b\bmod6&\eta_N(5)&\eta_N(7)&\eta_N(13)\\\hline
0&2&-1&0\\
1&-1&1&-1\\
2&-1&0&-2\\
3&-1&-1&0\\
4&-1&-2&2\\
5&2&-3&1
\end{array}                                                   \tag{7}
\]
There is no mass at $11$. To prove periodicity, increase $b$ and $a$ by $6$.
The cutoffs multiply by $64$, while $q_{a+6}=64q_a+21$ and
$M_{a+6}=64M_a$. Since $n_i-q_a$ is odd, the subtraction of
$21/(64M_a)$ does not change its quotient's floor. Also the shifted sources
are $64x_t+21\equiv x_t\pmod3$, so their coefficients are unchanged.
Thus each old valuation has the same lift count and residual coefficients
at the shifted valuation.
The six new valuations $a=1,\ldots,6$ contribute zero in total: odd $a$ have
no leftovers, and even $a$ contribute the vectors $(-2,1),(1,-2),(1,1)$
on $(7,13)$, which sum to zero. Direct arithmetic at $b=3,\ldots,8$ gives (7).

Since $\langle1,\nu_{2^b}\rangle=-1$, substitution of the four core hitting
times into (7) gives the sharper transfer bound
\[
\boxed{\quad
H_{T-1}(\PiR_{2^b})-12\le3\mathcal E_T(2^b)
\le H_{T-1}(\PiR_{2^b})+1,\quad T\ge1.
\quad}                                                       \tag{8}
\]
The equivalence above quantifies over every integer cutoff. The dyadic
computation alone does not establish its hypothesis; the next section proves
a separate sampling theorem.

\section{A sparse universal target: five dyadic rays}
\begin{theorem}[Five rays]\label{thm:rays}
Let $(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)=(12,14,16,18,21)$ and
$\mathcal G=\{a_i2^b:0\le i\le4,\ b\ge3\}$.
Each of Statements 2--4 of Theorem 3, restricted to $N\in\mathcal G$,
is equivalent to Collatz.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All odd seeds below $96$ terminate, as checked by exact finite iteration
in the accompanying script. Equations (5)--(6) give
$B(N)\le\Lip B(N)$ at each allowed cutoff under the bounded-work hypothesis.
For $i+3=5d+j$, $0\le j\le4$, put
$U=a_j2^{b+d}$. Then $U\ge3N/2$ when $N=a_i2^b$, because
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
a_i&12&14&16&18&21\\\hline
a_j2^d&18&21&24&28&32\\
3a_i/2&18&21&24&27&63/2
\end{array}
\]
Every $N$ is divisible by $8$. Monotonicity therefore gives
\[
B(N)\le B(N/4)+\tfrac13B(U)+\tfrac13B(U/2).                 \tag{9}
\]
Each argument on the right belongs to $\mathcal G$ or lies below $96$.
Define a comparison weight on the rays by
\[
F(a_i2^b)=2^{(5b+i)/3}.
\]
Use the same formula for the few smaller ray points on the right of (9).
The ray representation is unique. Since $5d+j-i=3$,
\[
F(U)=2F(N),\quad F(U/2)=2^{1-5/3}F(N),\quad
F(N/4)=2^{-10/3}F(N).
\]
Thus the comparison contracts by
\[
\lambda=2^{-10/3}+\tfrac23(1+2^{-5/3})
<\tfrac1{10}+\tfrac89=\tfrac{89}{90}<1,                     \tag{10}
\]
using $2^{10}>10^3$ and $2^5>3^3$.

If $B$ is nonzero, it is positive at some allowed cutoff because the grid
is unbounded. Also $B(N)/F(N)\to0$ along the grid, since
$B(N)\le N$ and $F(N)$ grows as a positive, ray-dependent constant times
$N^{5/3}$. The ratio therefore attains a positive global maximum $C$ at
an allowed cutoff. For arguments below $96$, $B=0\le CF$.
Applying (9)--(10) at the maximizer gives
$B(N)\le C\lambda F(N)<CF(N)=B(N)$, a contradiction.
Conversely, Collatz supplies exact finite currents for every finite measure.
The implications from currents to bounded work are unchanged.
\end{proof}
The comparison depends on the ray as well as the scale. Replacing it by a
single power of $N$ loses this five-state contraction. No infinite ray has
yet been certified, and no minimality claim for the number of rays is made.

\section{Disjoint annular increments under a factor of 64}
\begin{theorem}[Exact annular recursion]\label{thm:annular}
If $8\mid N$ and $Q=9N/4$, then
\[
\PiR_{64N}=\PiR_N+A_N,\qquad \eta_{64N}=\eta_N,
\]
where $\supp\PiR_N\subset(1,Q)$ and $\supp A_N\subset(Q,64Q)$.
For odd $y$ in $2^jQ<y<2^{j+1}Q$, the coefficient $A_N(y)$ depends only
on $j$ and $y\bmod18$:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
j\backslash(y\bmod18)&1&5&7&11&13&17\\\hline
0&1&0&1&0&-2&0\\
1&2&1&-1&1&-1&-2\\
2&3&-1&-3&2&0&-1\\
3&1&-3&-2&3&1&0\\
4&-1&-2&-1&1&2&1\\
5&0&-1&0&-1&0&2
\end{array}
\]
All other coefficients are zero. Each band has signed mass zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The cutoffs at $64N$ are $64n_i(N)$. For each old valuation, shifting
$a$ to $a+6$ replaces $q_a,M_a$ by $64q_a+21,64M_a$.
Since $n_i$ is even and $q_a$ is odd, this preserves the last lift index:
subtracting $21/64$ cannot cross the nearest lower multiple of $M_a$.
The shifted source is $64x+21\equiv x\pmod3$, so its coefficient is also
preserved. Thus the old packets and leftovers occur unchanged among
valuations $7,\ldots,b+6$ at the new cutoff.

For the six new valuations, write $N=8k$. In each component the odd
valuations $1,3,5$ have lift counts divisible by $3$. The even valuations
$2,4,6$ leave two lifts per component; the combined coefficients on
$(7,13)$ are $(-2,1),(1,-2),(1,1)$, respectively. Their sum is zero.
This proves the remainder identity and identifies $A_N$ with the full
pushforward contribution of the new six valuations.

For a possible endpoint $y>1$ and a valuation $a\le6$, the source is
$x=(2^ay-1)/3$; it is integral exactly when $2^ay\equiv1\pmod3$.
Its component coefficients are
$3\mathbf1_{x\equiv2\ (3)}-1$ and
$3\mathbf1_{x\equiv1\ (3)}-1$.
The corresponding endpoint cutoffs are $2^{7-a}Q$ and $2^{6-a}Q$.
These cutoffs are even, so no odd endpoint lies on a band boundary.
Below $Q$, all three allowed valuations of either parity occur in each
component. Their source residues cycle through all classes modulo $3$,
and their coefficients sum to zero. Above $64Q$, none occurs.
Within band $j$, the first component includes $a\le6-j$, and the second
includes $a\le5-j$, subject to the integrality condition. Evaluation modulo
$9$ gives the displayed table. Since $Q=18k$, each band comprises a whole
number of periods, and every row of the table sums to zero.

Finally, any original endpoint is at most $(3n_1(N)+1)/2=Q+1/2$.
It is odd and $Q$ is even, so it lies below $Q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A concrete infinite construction target]
It suffices to construct finite currents with nonpositive residual for
$A_{a_i2^{r+6k}}$, for every $i=0,\ldots,4$, $r=3,\ldots,8$, and $k\ge0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The 30 base measures $\PiR_{a_i2^r}$ have cutoffs at most $5376$ and
endpoints below $12096$. Exact finite iteration verifies convergence of
every odd seed below this endpoint bound within 98 odd steps. Therefore
each base has the finite exact current $\sum_{j=0}^{97}P^j\PiR_{a_i2^r}$.
Along each of the 30 ladders, sum this base current and the finitely
many increment certificates to obtain one for each $\PiR_N$ in
Theorem \ref{thm:rays}. Nonpositive residuals remain nonpositive under
addition. The theorem then gives Collatz.
\end{proof}
The base existence claim uses finite verified absorption; the 30 full
current tables have not been stored. The infinitely many increments remain
uncertified.
The annular recursion is an identity of measures; it supplies no current
for $A_N$ and no decreasing termination parameter.

\section{What finite currents detect}
The following observation applies to any deterministic map with one
absorber, with pushforward killed there. Two states are in the same
\emph{orbit component} when their forward trajectories meet, allowing
unequal arrival times. A component is absorbing if its states reach the
absorber.

\begin{theorem}[Component criterion]
For a finite signed measure $\mu$, let $m_C$ be its total mass in an
orbit component $C$. A finite exact current for $\mu$ exists if and only if
$m_C=0$ in every nonabsorbing component. A finite current with nonpositive
residual exists if and only if $m_C\le0$ in each such component.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every nonabsorbing component indicator is invariant, so its pairing with
a finite divergence vanishes. This proves necessity.
For sufficiency, in each component meeting $\supp\mu$, route the finitely
many sources to a common forward descendant, with arbitrary arrival
times. In the absorbing component route them to the absorber. The sum of
these finite path currents has residual $m_C\delta_{z_C}$ at each chosen
nonabsorbing descendant $z_C$, which proves both assertions.
\end{proof}

For one fixed measure, bounded work is weaker: beyond the largest finite
source hitting time,
\[
H_L(\mu)=L\sum_{C\ {\rm nonabsorbing}}m_C+
          \sum_{\tau(x)<\infty}\mu(x)\tau(x).
\]
It only requires the \emph{sum} of nonabsorbing masses to be nonpositive.
For example, on negative odd integers the cycles $-1\mapsto-1$ and
$-5\mapsto-7\mapsto-5$ give $H_L(\delta_{-1}-\delta_{-5})=0$,
but no such current exists. This example is outside the positive Collatz
domain; it does not claim a positive nonterminating orbit.

A complete semidecision search exposes progressively longer paths from all
sources, retaining historical intersections. In each finite undirected
component of exposed arrows, route source mass along a spanning tree to
one anchor (the absorber when present). Traversing an arrow backwards
uses a negative current coefficient. If all remaining component masses
are zero, or nonpositive in the one-sided version, this is a certificate.
The component proof supplies finite witness paths whenever a certificate
exists, so sufficiently deep exposure succeeds. A finite depth cap may
return unresolved. Universal termination remains unproved.

\begin{theorem}[No bounded per-packet catalogue]
For every integer $H\ge0$, put $K=2H+7$, $y=9\cdot2^K-1$, and
$N=4\cdot2^K+5$. The actual final valuation-one packet in the first
source component at this cutoff is
\[
\mu_y=2\delta_y-\delta_{y+6}-\delta_{y+12}.
\]
Any finite exact current for $\mu_y$, or one with nonpositive residual,
has more than $H$ nonzero rows, including when arbitrary auxiliary states
are allowed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At valuation one the lifts are $x_t=3+4t$, $y_t=5+6t$.
For $t=3\cdot2^{K-1}-1$, the last source index is $t+2$.
Right alignment leaves two initial lifts, and the final coefficients are
$(2,-1,-1)$ because $t\equiv2\pmod3$.

The three starting points are perturbations by $9\cdot2^K$ of $-1,5,11$.
Their base trajectories are the fixed point $-1$ and
$5\to1$, $11\to17\to13\to5\to1$. Use the usual formula, fixing $1$,
to describe these base trajectories only. Their accumulated valuations
$A_j$ obey $A_j\le2H+2$ for $0\le j\le H$. Exact branch arithmetic gives
\[
S^j(z_0+9\cdot2^K)=z_j+9\cdot3^j2^{K-A_j}.
\]
At least five bits remain in the perturbation. The first orbit is therefore
$31\bmod32$, whereas the other two are in $\{1,5,11,13,17\}\bmod32$,
through time $H$. They cannot meet each other across these two groups,
even at unequal times, and the positive orbit cannot reach $1$.

If a current had at most $H$ nonzero arrows, its finite undirected component
containing $y$ would have to contain a negative source or the absorber:
otherwise summing the divergence identity there would give
$2=\sum_C\zeta\le0$. A path to such a vertex uses at most $H$ arrows.
In a deterministic graph, an undirected simple path is directed toward a
common meeting vertex, since an internal vertex cannot have two outgoing
arrows. Its endpoints therefore meet with each arrival time at most $H$,
contradicting the established separation.
\end{proof}
The obstruction concerns individual packets. It leaves open cancellation
between packets and recursively expanded certificates of unbounded size.

\section{Universal partial currents for the annuli}
Throughout this section $8\mid N$ and $Q=9N/4$. All coefficient claims
refer to the actual six-band measure $A_N$ of Theorem \ref{thm:annular}.

\begin{lemma}[Affine lifting of a finite current tree]\label{lem:affine}
Let a finite directed tree of positive odd Syracuse states have common
sink $w$. For a vertex $x$, let $d_x$ and $A_x$ be the path length and
accumulated valuation to $w$, and put $D=\max_x d_x$. For integer $t\ge0$
define
\[
x(t)=x+2^{A_x+1}3^{D-d_x}t.
\]
Every tree edge remains an exact Syracuse edge after this substitution.
If integer source weights $\mu$ have total $m\le0$, route them to $w$,
obtaining a finite current $J$. The same coefficients at the lifted
vertices satisfy
\[
\mu_t=(I-P)J_t+m\delta_{w(t)},
\]
where the last term is discarded when $w(t)=1$. In particular, $m=0$
gives an exact current for all $t$, with positive cost at most
$\sum_x\max(J(x),0)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For an edge $x\to y$ of valuation $a$,
$d_x=d_y+1$ and $A_x=a+A_y$. The slopes $v_x$ therefore satisfy
$3v_x=2^av_y$, whence $3x(t)+1=2^ay(t)$. Every lifted state is odd,
so the valuation remains exactly $a$. Summing the path currents proves
the divergence identity. Coincident lifted states are combined by
linearity, which cannot increase positive cost. No continuation from the
lifted sink is needed, even if the original sink was $1$.
\end{proof}
The lemma proves an identity. Its source coefficients must separately be
shown to occur in $A_N$ without reuse.

\subsection{A short-chain catalogue}
Put $x_1=(3x+1)/2$ and $x_2=(9x+5)/4$. For each row below, take every
integer $x$ in the strict interval and listed classes modulo $72$.
These classes are all $7\bmod8$, so the first two valuations equal $1$.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
 &x/N&x\bmod72&
 (\mu(x),\mu(x_1),\mu(x_2))&(J(x),J(x_1))\\ \hline
E&(12,16)&7&(-3,3,0)&(-3,0)\\

Every nonzero source coefficient in this table is the whole coefficient

To check this directly, their state intervals in units of $N$, with
residues modulo $18$, are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 &x&x_1&x_2\\ \hline
E&(12,16):7&(18,24):11&--\\

Intervals or residues separate the columns, and each affine map is
injective. The strict endpoints are valid since $8\mid N$ and the
permitted residues exclude boundary crossings from the affine offsets.
For weights $(c_0,c_1,c_2)$ summing to zero the current is
$c_0\delta_x+(c_0+c_1)\delta_{x_1}$. Thus their summed measure

M_N^{\rm cat}=(I-P)J_N^{\rm cat},\qquad J_N^{\rm cat}\le0.
\]
Define the exact counts

C(a,b;r)=\left\lfloor\frac{bN-1-r}{72}\right\rfloor
          -\left\lfloor\frac{aN-r}{72}\right\rfloor .
\]
The removed positive mass is
\begin{align*}
D(N)={}&3C(12,16;7)+C(16,18;23)+C(16,18;71)\\
 &+2C(24,32;7)+3C(32,36;23)\\
 &+2C(48,64;7)+2C(48,64;55).
\end{align*}
In particular $D(N)=3N/2$ when $72\mid N$. In general
$D(N)-3N/2$ is periodic modulo $72$ on the allowed cutoffs. The remaining
positive mass is $99N/4-D(N)$, so this is a partial certificate.

\subsection{Packing an unequal-time cancellation tree}
An actual balanced component found in $A_{96}$ has paths
\[
6121\to4591\to6887\to10331\to15497\to11623\to17435,

\[
13775\to20663\to30995\to46493\to17435.

Its source coefficients at $(4591,6121,6887,10331,13775)$ are
$(-1,-1,1,2,-1)$. Lemma \ref{lem:affine} gives the exact current family
with source

\begin{split}
\mu_t={}&-\delta_{4591+384t}-\delta_{6121+512t}
 +\delta_{6887+576t}\\
 &+2\delta_{10331+864t}-\delta_{13775+1152t}.
\end{split}

The current coefficients on the ten non-sink tree vertices, in increasing
order, are $(-2,-1,-1,1,1,-1,1,-1,-1,-1)$; its positive cost is $3$.

Take $t=9s+r$, $s\ge0$, $r\in\{0,3\}$. For $8\mid N$, all five source
coefficients occur in $A_N$ exactly under the conditions
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
r&\text{admissible cutoff}&
 \text{source residues modulo }18\\ \hline
0&96+72s\le N\le120+96s&(1,1,11,17,5)\\
3&120+72s\le N\le152+96s&(1,7,11,17,5).
\end{array}

The first three sources lie in $(36N,72N)$ and the last two in
$(72N,144N)$. Substituting the affine formulas into these ten strict
inequalities gives the stated integer bounds; the first and last
sources give the tight upper and lower bounds.
Let $I_r(N)$ be the set of admissible $s$ in row $r$, and put
$C(N)=|I_0(N)|+|I_3(N)|$. All admissible copies can be packed together:
their ten arithmetic source progressions have offsets $x_i+rv_i$
and steps $9v_i$, and for each of the 45 pairs the gcd of the steps
does not divide the offset difference. Thus distinct copies use
disjoint source states. In particular,
\[
C(N)=N/144+O(1).
\]
There is at least one copy for $96\le N\le152$ and for every $N\ge168$
with $8\mid N$; at $N=160$ this particular packing is empty.

These sources are also disjoint from the short-chain catalogue.
The first source is below $48N$ and has residue $1\bmod18$;
the second has residue $1$ or $25\bmod72$, excluding catalogue

above $48N$. The fourth lies in the unused residue-$17$ gap
$(81N,108N)$, and the fifth has the unused top-band residue $5$.
These statements follow directly from the admissibility intervals.
Consequently both partial constructions may be added.

\begin{theorem}[A universal combined partial current]\label{thm:partial}
For every $8\mid N$, the two preceding constructions give explicit
finite integer measures $J_N,Z_N$ with
\[
A_N=(I-P)J_N+Z_N,\qquad
\|J_N^+\|_1\le3C(N),\qquad
\|Z_N^+\|_1=\frac{99N}{4}-D(N)-3C(N).
\]
Here $Z_N$ is exactly $A_N$ with the disjoint certified source atoms
deleted. The removed positive mass is $73N/48+O(1)$.
The remaining positive mass has no general certificate yet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Sum the exact currents over all the disjoint sources proved above.
The catalogue current is nonpositive and the packed current has positive
cost at most $3C(N)$. Exact deletion gives the residual formula.
\end{proof}
Zero positive cost cannot be the complete target: direct exact iteration
gives $H_{53}(A_{96})=699$ and $H_9(A_{384})=42$. Hence a complete
current with nonpositive residual for $A_{96}$ has positive cost at least
$699$.

\section{An exact one-pass compression}
Let $F(x)=4x+1$, so $S(F(x))=S(x)$. Reduce an odd $x$ repeatedly by
$x\mapsto(x-1)/4$ while $x\equiv5\pmod8$, obtaining its root $\rho(x)$.
This procedure terminates by strict decrease. Let $R\mu$ collect the
coefficients at these roots and discard the root $1$.
Then $PR\mu=P\mu$, and
\[
\mu=(I-P)(\mu-R\mu)+R\mu.                              \tag{11}
\]
Different roots have different successors: the root preceding $y$ is
$(2y-1)/3$ if $y\equiv2\pmod3$, or $(4y-1)/3$ if
$y\equiv1\pmod3$. Root $1$ is the only discarded class.

\begin{theorem}[Annular norm formula]\label{thm:norm}
Let $c_N$ be the signed annular mass on the $F$-chain rooted at $1$.
For every $8\mid N$,
\[
\|RA_N\|_1=\|PA_N\|_1=\frac{171N}{4}-|c_N|,
\qquad |c_N|\le3.
\]
Thus $\|RA_N\|_1\le(19/22)\|A_N\|_1$. Formula (11) gives an explicit
finite current realizing this compression.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $f_j$ denote row $j$ of the annular residue table. Each $F$-chain
has a first point in the annulus, its entry. All odd points in bands
$0,1$ are entries; in bands $2,\ldots,5$ the entries are precisely those
not congruent to $5\bmod8$. Successive annular points from an entry in
band $j$ lie in bands $j,j+2,\ldots$. Their signed sums
$g_j(x)=\sum_{0\le k\le(5-j)/2}f_{j+2k}(F^k(x)\bmod18)$ are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrrr}
j\backslash r&1&3&5&7&9&11&13&15&17\\ \hline
0&0&1&2&3&1&-1&-3&-2&-1\\
1&-1&3&1&2&0&1&-1&-3&-2\\
2&1&2&-1&-2&-1&2&1&-1&-1\\
3&0&0&-3&-3&0&3&3&0&0\\
4&-1&0&-2&-1&0&1&2&0&1\\
5&0&0&-1&0&0&-1&0&0&2
\end{array}
\]
The absolute sums in one period $18$ for bands $0,1$ are $14,14$.
Including the entry condition gives absolute sums $36,36,24,12$
in each period $72$ for bands $2,\ldots,5$. The relevant band boundaries
are exact multiples of their periods. The six contributions are therefore
$N(7/4,7/2,9/2,9,12,12)$, summing to $171N/4$.
The root-$1$ chain enters band $0$ or $1$, so the first two rows bound
its coefficient by $3$. Removing it subtracts $|c_N|$.
Injectivity of the successor on the remaining roots proves the equality
with $\|PA_N\|_1$.
\end{proof}
The correction can vanish: $c_{80000}=0$. More importantly, the residual
is not another annulus, and $R^2=R$, so the contraction cannot be iterated
as stated. It leaves coefficient $+2$ at each
$32Q<p<64Q$ with $p\equiv-1\pmod{36}$: these are roots and have no
further $F$-point in the annulus. There are exactly $2N$ of them, retaining
positive mass at least $4N$.

\section{The remaining component progress problem}
For a finite graph $E$ of exposed Syracuse arrows, with all source
vertices and the absorber included, put
\[
\Phi(E)=\sum_{\substack{C\ {\rm component\ of}\ E\\1\notin C}}
 \max\left(\sum_{x\in C}A_N(x),0\right).
\]
This nonnegative integer never increases as arrows are added:
components merge and $\max(u+v,0)\le\max(u,0)+\max(v,0)$.
It is zero exactly when a current supported on $E$ can have
nonpositive residual. An indicator $q$ of a positive component is an
exact obstruction on this graph, since $q(x)=q(Sx)$ on every exposed
arrow while $\langle q,A_N\rangle>0$.

The missing arithmetic statement is that every positive plateau of
$\Phi$ ends after finitely many permitted extensions. Monotonicity alone
does not imply this. For the actual $A_{96}$, exposing the first $d$
arrows from every source gives $\Phi(E_0)=2376$,
$\Phi(E_d)=17$ for $20\le d\le27$, and $\Phi(E_{28})=0$.
These are finite certificates, not a bound for arbitrary $N$.

There is also a precise boundary term in attempts to lift the scalar
scale recurrence. Let $U_t$ count odd states $1<x\le t$,
$F_t=U_{64t}-U_t$, and set
\[
\begin{split}
V_N&=\nu_{64N}-\nu_N,\qquad K_N=V_N+3(F_N-F_{N/4}),\\
D_N&=3F_N-3F_{N/4}-F_{3N/2}-F_{3N/4}.
\end{split}
\]
The exact source identity
$\nu_N=3P(U_N-U_{N/4})-U_{3N/2}-U_{3N/4}$, valid for $8\mid N$,
and $A_N=PV_N$ give
\[
D_N=A_N+(I-P)K_N.
\]
Thus a finite-component indicator $q$ with $q(1)=0$ satisfies
\[
\langle q,D_N\rangle=\langle q,A_N\rangle+
 \sum_x K_N(x)\bigl(q(x)-q(Sx)\bigr).                    \tag{12}
\]
The last sum is supported on unexposed arrows. Global invariance kills
it; invariance on a finite exposed graph does not.
At depth $27$ for $A_{96}$, the component containing $1819$ has
$83$ vertices and $31$ source atoms. Its pairings and flux are
$10=17-7$ in (12). Even exposing every row of $K_{96}$ leaves a
positive component of mass $17$, now with zero flux. Hence boundary
closure at one cutoff also needs a progress proof.

Two further scoped obstructions are proved in the companion notes.
For fixed $k\ge1$ and $2^{k+2}\mid N$, the source in each band projects
to zero modulo $2^k$ and modulo $3\cdot2^k$; these projections lose
the integer matching information. Under literal forward iteration,
the outer coefficient profile instead requires growing ternary moduli.
Neither observation rules out arithmetic rewrites, additional state
information, or historical cancellation.

\subsection{Why interleaving collapse and transport does not close the gap}
The operator relations $R^2=R$ and $PR=P$ reduce every word in $P,R$
to $I$, $R$, $P^t$, or $RP^t$. In particular,
\[
(RP)^tR\mu=RP^t\mu,\qquad
\|RP^t\mu\|_1=\|P^{t+1}\mu\|_1.
\]
On root states greater than $1$, the bijection $\phi(x)=S(x)$ to odd
states greater than $1$ coprime to $3$ conjugates the killed map
$x\mapsto\rho(S(x))$ to killed Syracuse. The inverse is
$(2y-1)/3$ for $y\equiv2\pmod3$ and $(4y-1)/3$ for
$y\equiv1\pmod3$. If the new root is $1$, both conjugate maps are killed.
Thus these operations only change the representatives of the same dynamics.

There is also an explicit surviving family. For fixed $t\ge0$, every
\[
72N<x<144N,\qquad x\equiv-1\pmod{9\cdot2^{t+2}}
\]
has, for $0\le j\le t$, the positive coefficient $2$ in both
$P^jA_N$ and $RP^jA_N$ at $y_j=3^j(x+1)/2^j-1$.
There are exactly
$\lfloor4N/2^t\rfloor-\lfloor2N/2^t\rfloor$ such sources.
All their first branches have valuation $1$. Any endpoint involving a
larger valuation lies below $(3/2)^j(72N+1/3)-1$, whereas the selected
points lie above this bound. Thus no other synchronous history cancels
them. They are already roots, and their next $4y+1$ representatives
exceed the entire transported support.

For $N=a2^b$, the top source $144N-1$ consequently survives through
$b+2$ transports at $324a3^b-1$. Hence no fixed transport depth or
uniform linear state-height bound closes this particular process.
These conclusions permit cancellation at unequal times. The proposed
repeated $19/22$ norm bound already fails at $N=96$:
$\|P^2A_{96}\|_1=3533$ and $\|P^3A_{96}\|_1=3124$.
A later reachable residual even has unchanged norm $13$ and positive
mass $4$ after one more transport and collapse.

An independently checked \emph{aggregate} example goes beyond synchronous
transport. At $N=12\cdot2^{27}$, the complete component of the top source
in the depth-$32$ historical exposure graph has $35$ vertices and exactly
the source atoms $(64N-1,-2)$, $(96N-1,2)$, $(144N-1,2)$.
Its mass is $2$, so no current supported on that entire graph has
nonpositive residual. A bounded inverse enumeration checks every possible
incoming exposed edge without materializing the full annulus.
This is a fixed finite-depth obstruction, not an arbitrary-depth theorem.
At $N=10368$, the depth-$2$ top component instead has $27$ source atoms
of total zero, illustrating why a lone growing orbit is insufficient.

\section{A finite compiler using the integer component gap}
The boundary problem can be handled conditionally across finitely many
scales. The next result converts supplied packet certificates into
ordinary absorption paths. It does not provide those inputs universally.

\begin{lemma}[Strict integer gap]\label{lem:integergap}
Let $\mu$ be a finite signed integer measure. If a finite rational current
satisfies
\[
\mu=(I-P)J+Z,\qquad \|Z^+\|_1<1,
\]
then the finite graph of its nonzero current arrows supports an integer
current for $\mu$ with nonpositive residual. Include all source states
and the absorber as vertices. If $\mu\ge0$, every nonzero source is in
the absorbing component, and the same graph supplies exact absorption
paths and an exact nonnegative integer current.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On a component $C$ not containing $1$, the divergence sums to zero.
Thus $\sum_C\mu=\sum_C Z\le\|Z^+\|_1<1$. The left side is an integer,
so it is nonpositive. Routing integer source masses to component anchors
gives the claimed current. If $\mu\ge0$, no unabsorbed component contains
a source. A finite functional component containing the sink $1$ cannot
contain another sink or directed cycle, so every source follows its
existing arrows to $1$.
\end{proof}
The strict threshold and source integrality are essential. In a general
functional graph, a unit source at a nonabsorbing fixed point has residual
mass $1$ and fails the conclusion; this supplies no positive Syracuse
fixed point. The lemma replaces a rational certificate by a different
integer one; it does not simply discard a positive residual in a work bound.

Enumerate the five-ray grid by $N_k=a_i2^b$, $k=5b+i\ge15$.
Use the same formula for the lower-boundary indices $5\le k<15$.
The rounded recurrence uses indices $k-10,k+3,k-2$. A rational comparison
weight is $f_k=r^k$, where
\[
r=\frac{35}{27},\qquad
\lambda=r^{-10}+\frac{r^3+r^{-2}}3
=\frac{162742333796706147551}{162889462677744140625}<1.
\]
Here $r^5>2$ and $a_i/r^i\le12$ for all $i$.
For a target integer $m\ge1$, let $k_*$ be the first grid index with
$g=N_{k_*}\ge m$. Choose the least integer $B\ge4$, $k_*<5B$, with
\[
6r^{k_*}(2/r^5)^B<1.
\]
This is a finite exact rational search. It specifies $5(B-3)$ packet
cutoffs, all $N_k$ with $15\le k\le K=5B-1$.

On these indices define a matrix $A$ with row transitions of weights
$1,1/3,1/3$ to $k-10,k+3,k-2$, omitting targets outside the block.
The weighted norm bound $Af\le\lambda f$ proves that $I-A$ has a
nonnegative inverse. The finite rational vector
\[
w=(I-A)^{-T}e_{k_*}\ge0
\]
is computed by Gaussian elimination. For every omitted target $\ell$,
let $c_\ell\ge0$ be the sum of its incoming weights multiplied by $w_k$.
Define
\[
\widetilde D_k=3U_{N_k}-3U_{N_{k-10}}-U_{N_{k+3}}-U_{N_{k-2}}.
\]
The transpose equation gives the exact finite measure identity
\[
U_g=\sum_{k=15}^{K}\frac{w_k}{3}\widetilde D_k
       +\sum_{\ell\notin[15,K]}c_\ell U_{N_\ell}.
\]
All lower-boundary cutoffs are below $96$. The upper-boundary mass is
\[
T=\sum_{\ell>K}c_\ell(N_\ell/2-1)<1.
\]
Indeed, the corresponding solution $h=Ah+b$ with zero lower boundary
and upper values $N_\ell/2-1$ satisfies the finite maximum principle for
$h_k/r^k$. Therefore
$T\le r^{k_*}\max_{\ell>K}(N_\ell/2-1)/r^\ell
<6r^{k_*}(2/r^5)^B$.
An exact solve can also establish $T<1$ for a smaller block.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional finite certificate compiler]\label{thm:finitecompiler}
Suppose finite currents with nonpositive residual are supplied for all
the selected $\PiR_{N_k}$. If the finite rational solve above has $T<1$,
their arrows, together with explicit source-scale currents and the known
finite base/core paths, construct an exact integer current for $U_g$.
Every odd seed at most $g$ is thereby certified to reach $1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $8\mid N$, put
$D_N=3U_N-3U_{N/4}-U_{3N/2}-U_{3N/4}$ and
$K_N=\nu_N+3(U_N-U_{N/4})$. The exact source transport gives
\[
D_N=\PiR_N+\eta_N+(I-P)K_N.
\]
The rounding shell
$W_k=(U_{N_{k+3}}-U_{3N_k/2})+
      (U_{N_{k-2}}-U_{3N_k/4})$ is nonnegative, and
$\widetilde D_k=D_{N_k}-W_k$.
Substitute the supplied identities
$\PiR_{N_k}=(I-P)J_k+\zeta_k$, $\zeta_k\le0$.
Absorb the lower-boundary measures and the signed core remainders by
their known finite path currents. The result is
$U_g=(I-P)J_*+Z$, where
\[
Z=\sum_k\frac{w_k}{3}\zeta_k-\sum_k\frac{w_k}{3}W_k
        +\sum_{\ell>K}c_\ell U_{N_\ell}.
\]
Thus $\|Z^+\|_1\le T<1$. Lemma \ref{lem:integergap} extracts the
integer current and actual paths without exposing further arrows.
All source-scale boundary terms have been retained as currents.
\end{proof}
With $p=5\log_2(35/27)\simeq1.87198$, the sufficient block contains
$O(\log m)$ cutoffs, the largest being $O(m^{p/(p-1)})$,
where $p/(p-1)\simeq2.14682$. This bounds which input certificates suffice,
not their size, cost, search time, or universal availability.

For $m=97$, $g=112$, the exact solve already works at $B=9$, using the
30 starting cutoffs through $5376$. Actual checked one-sided input
currents combine into a rational current with denominator $46422343855805$
and positive residual numerator $29145292346068$. The strict gap
extracts a stored $95$-row nonnegative integer current for $U_{112}$,
with no new arrows. Twenty input certificates still had sources unlinked
to $1$ in their individual discovery graphs.
This is a verified finite conversion. Using it for arbitrary residuals
can require certificates at larger cutoffs, so a decreasing induction
cannot assume those inputs without a separate construction.

\section{An infinite family of complete depth-32 obstructions}
The earlier finite obstruction extends to an infinite parameter family.
Let $G_D(N)$ expose the first $D$ arrows from every nonzero source of
$A_N$, retaining all historical intersections and stopping at $1$.

\begin{theorem}[Complete symbolic component lift]\label{thm:symboliclift}
Put $N_0=96\cdot2^{24}$. For every integer $q\ge1$ with
$q\equiv1\pmod{3^{22}}$, set $N=N_0q$. The component of $144N-1$ in the
entire graph $G_{32}(N)$ is precisely the chain
\[
 x_j=64N(3/2)^j-1,\qquad 0\le j\le34.
\]
Its only source coefficients are $(-2,2,2)$ at $j=0,1,2$.
It has mass $2$ and does not contain $1$. Consequently it obstructs every
current with nonpositive residual supported on this exposure graph.
In particular this holds on the infinite required-ray subfamily
\[
 N=12\cdot2^{27+20920706406\,k},\qquad k\ge0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof by exact finite symbolic certificate]
Represent every inverse-query state as $x(q)=aq+b$, with
$a,b\in\mathbb Z[1/3]$. All slopes are positive with even numerator.
An inverse branch sends $(a,b)$ to $(2^v a/3,(2^v b-1)/3)$.
The complete trace has largest denominator $3^{20}$, so
$q\equiv1\pmod{3^{22}}$ preserves integrality and the full residue
modulo $18$ of every queried state.

All source-band boundaries and forward height bounds are affine in $q$;
the latter are $3^r144N_0q/2^r-1$, $0\le r\le32$.
Each required inequality is certified as $f(q)=f(1)+u(q-1)$,
where $u\ge0$ and $f(1)$ has the required weak or strict sign.
This includes the first excluded inverse predecessor, hence all larger
predecessors of the same valuation parity. Induction on the remaining
depth proves every inverse reachability decision for the whole family.

The reproducible exact certificate checks $9171$ such decisions and
$42487$ affine inequalities. At each displayed vertex its complete
incoming exposed list is just the previous chain vertex, when present;
its outgoing arrow is exposed exactly for $j<34$. Every edge has
valuation one. All source-band and residue decisions give the stated
three coefficients. Thus the certified chain is the entire component.
The finite-component criterion proves the obstruction.
Finally $2^{2\cdot3^{21}}\equiv1\pmod{3^{22}}$, by repeatedly cubing
$4\equiv1\pmod3$, gives the dyadic subfamily.
\end{proof}

The proof trace, its exact hashes, independent integer comparisons, and
the full lifting argument are in
\texttt{experiments/residual\_progress/templates/}.
This symbolic lift alone keeps the depth fixed at $32$.
The next argument removes that limitation.

\section{No fixed exposure depth handles all actual annuli}
\begin{theorem}[Unbounded aggregate exposure depth]\label{thm:unboundeddepth}
For each integer $D\ge1$, choose a positive integer $X$ such that
\[
 2^{\max(D+4,10)}\mid X,\qquad X\equiv1\pmod{3^{D+2}},
 \qquad N=3X/128.
\]
Then the complete component of $144N-1$ in $G_D(N)$ is
\[
 y_k=(3/2)^kX-1,\qquad 1\le k\le D+3,
\]
with consecutive arrows and only source coefficients $(-2,2,2)$
at $k=1,2,3$. Its mass $2$ obstructs every one-sided current on $G_D(N)$.
For every $D$ there are infinitely many such cutoffs on one required
base-64 ladder:
\[
 N=12\cdot2^{(2\cdot3^{D+1})t-9}
   =96\cdot64^{3^Dt-2},\qquad t\ge1.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The divisibility makes all displayed states odd and all consecutive
valuations equal to one. Their first three residues modulo $18$ are
$5,17,17$, giving the stated coefficients; later vertices are above
the annulus. Every displayed arrow is exposed from one of those three
sources within $D$ steps. The final vertex exceeds the maximum height
reachable from any source in $D-1$ steps, so its outgoing arrow is absent.

We exclude all extra incoming histories. Reverse an extra incoming branch
at $y_k$ through $l\le D$ steps with valuations $a_1,\ldots,a_l$, and put
$A=\sum a_i$. Write its state and its auxiliary value at $X=1$ as
\[
 x=uX-c=u(X-1)+z,\qquad
 u=3^{k-l}2^{A-k},\quad c>0,\quad z=u-c.
\]
An inverse step updates $u'=2^a u/3$, $c'=(2^a c+1)/3$,
and $z'=(2^a z-1)/3$.
The first extra inverse valuation is odd and at least $3$, so
$E=A-l\ge2$ thereafter.

Before the first integer auxiliary value, $A<k$, $l<k$, and $c$ stays
a positive odd integer: actual integrality and the available power of
$2$ in $X$ enforce $3\mid2^a c+1$ at each step. The auxiliary values
are strictly greater than the all-ones comparison
$(3/2)^{k-l}-1\ge0$. With $s=k-A>0$ one has
$u=3^E(3/2)^s\ge9$.

At first integer arrival, $r=k-l\ge0$ and $s=A-k\ge0$ satisfy
$r+s=E\ge2$, hence $u=3^r2^s\ge4$ and $z$ is a positive integer.
If $z$ is even, $s=0$, $r\ge2$, and $u\ge9$; the next admissible
inverse gives an odd auxiliary value and $u'\ge6$.
For odd auxiliary values maintain
\[
 c>0,\qquad u\ge4,\qquad z=5\Longrightarrow u\ge6.
\]
These hold at the first odd arrival. Throughout histories of length
at most $D$, the congruence on $X$ makes $u(X-1)$ an integer divisible
by $3$ after integer arrival. Thus actual inverse admissibility also
enforces integer auxiliary updates. An inverse with $a\ge2$ increases
$u$. For $a=1$, an admissible positive odd $z$ is $5$ or at least
$11$: the special condition handles $5$, and $u>z$ handles the rest.
A new auxiliary value $5$ can only come from $z=1$, $a=4$, giving
$u'\ge64/3$. This proves the invariant.

Every extra-branch ancestor therefore has $u\ge4$ and $z>0$, so
\[
 x>4(X-1)>(27/8)X-1=144N-1
\]
because $X\ge1024$. None can be an annular source. An extra incoming
exposed arrow would require such a source within $D$ inverse steps,
which is impossible. The remaining bottom predecessor $X-1$ is
divisible by $3$, has zero annular coefficient, and has no predecessor.
This exhausts all incoming edges and proves complete component closure.

Finally choose $X=2^{(2\cdot3^{D+1})t}$. Repeated cubing of
$4\equiv1\pmod3$ proves the ternary congruence, and its exponent
supplies the required binary divisibility and ray exponent at least $3$.
\end{proof}

If $d_*(N)$ is the least sufficient exposure depth, with infinity allowed,
this gives
\[
 d_*(96\cdot64^k)\ge v_3(k+2)+1,\qquad k\ge0.
\]
For $v_3(k+2)=0$ the bound follows from the positive source atoms in the
graph with no arrows. A finite catalogue of source-covered common-tail
trees has a finite maximum source-to-anchor depth, so the theorem
excludes every such catalogue, even with cross-packet cancellation and
fractional combinations. Recursive use with unbounded total depth remains
possible. No nonexistence of a full finite current at a particular
cutoff is asserted.

\section{What packet inputs supply locally}
The finite compiler's inputs have a precise relation to ordinary
absorption on an inner interval. In this section $8\mid N$, and $U_t$
counts odd states $1<x\le t$. Use the single-cutoff measures
\[
 D_N=3U_N-3U_{N/4}-U_{3N/2}-U_{3N/4},\qquad
 K_N=\nu_N+3(U_N-U_{N/4}).
\]
The exact identity already used by the compiler is
$D_N=\PiR_N+\eta_N+(I-P)K_N$.
Write
\[
 \mathcal B_N=2U_N-U_{N/4}-U_{3N/4},\qquad
 \mathcal L_N=2U_{N/4}+U_{3N/2}-U_N.
\]
Both are nonnegative, $D_N=\mathcal B_N-\mathcal L_N$,
$\supp\mathcal B_N=(N/4,N]\cap(2\mathbb Z+1)$, and their masses are
$N/2$ and $N/2-2$, respectively.

\begin{theorem}[Local calibration and retained residual]\label{thm:localinput}
Suppose a finite nonnegative absorption current $H$ is supplied for
$\mathcal B_N$, and let $E$ be the fixed-core current
$(I-P)E=\eta_N$. Then
\[
 \PiR_N=(I-P)(H-K_N-E)-\mathcal L_N
\]
is an automatic one-sided packet certificate.
For any supplied $\PiR_N=(I-P)J+\zeta$, $\zeta\le0$, the converse
conversion is
\[
 \mathcal L_N=(I-P)(H-K_N-E-J)-\zeta.
\]
If $\zeta=0$, the same finite graph supplies actual absorption paths
for every odd seed in the low quarter and the outer shell $(N,3N/2]$.
For a one-sided input the nonnegative residual $-\zeta$ is retained.
On the graph of the converted current, with residual and source vertices
included, one has exactly
\[
 2\,\#\{\text{unabsorbed low-quarter seeds}\}
 +\#\{\text{unabsorbed outer-shell seeds}\}
 =-\sum_{\text{nonabsorbing components}}\zeta
 \le\|\zeta^-\|_1.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $(I-P)H=\mathcal B_N$ and $(I-P)E=\eta_N$ into the
source-scale identity. For the counting statement, sum the converted
identity over every nonabsorbing exposed component. Divergences cancel.
If the residual vanishes, nonnegativity of $\mathcal L_N$ forces every
source into the absorbing component; its actual paths already occur
in that finite graph.
\end{proof}

Feeding the automatic input $J=H-K_N-E$, $\zeta=-\mathcal L_N$
back into the converse gives zero current and the unchanged residual
$\mathcal L_N$. This algebraic composition supplies no smaller residual.
Useful extra paths in the retained union of input graphs would require
a separate argument.
The largest raw packet source is therefore not an intrinsic absorption
dependency: the explicit $K_N$ removes it. The unknown inner interval
is the relevant dependency of this particular construction.

There is also an exact limit on adaptive positive source-scale cones.
For an odd $x>1$,
\[
 D_N(x)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\le N<4x.
\]
Indeed its value is $2$ for $x\le N<4x/3$, $1$ for
$4x/3\le N<4x$, and is nonpositive elsewhere.
In any identity
$U_g=\sum_N\alpha_ND_N+V-W+Z$, with $g\ge x$,
$\alpha_N\ge0$, $\supp V\subset(1,x)$, $W\ge0$, and
$\|Z^+\|_1<1$, evaluation at $x$ therefore requires a positive-weight
cutoff $N\in[x,4x)$. Thus such a cone cannot supply a new seed using
only the automatic inputs whose entire inner intervals are below that
seed. This is a restriction on the stated cone and input construction;
independent arithmetic, favorable supplied residuals, or orbit
cancellations remain possible.

\section{A terminating partial normalization with auxiliary sources}
An explicit backward rewrite gives a decreasing parameter, but its
terminal positive mass has a proved obstruction.
For a positive coefficient $c$ at $y$, use the following ancestors:
\[
 q(y)=
 \begin{cases}
 (2y-1)/3,&y\equiv5,11\pmod{18},\\
 (8y-5)/9,&y\equiv13\pmod{18},\\
 (4y-5)/9,&y\equiv17\pmod{18}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The latter two paths have intermediate vertices $(4y-1)/3$ and
$(2y-1)/3$, and forward valuations $(1,2)$ and $(1,1)$.
The first path has valuation one. Replacing $c\delta_y$ by
$c\delta_{q(y)}$ adds the current $-c$ along that path.
Every endpoint obeys $3\le q(y)<8y/9<y$.

\begin{theorem}[Unique terminating partial normalization]\label{thm:auxnormal}
For any finite integer source $\mu$, these positive-coefficient
replacements terminate with an order-independent current and residual
\[
 \mu=(I-P)J(\mu)+Z(\mu),\qquad J(\mu)\le0.
\]
Every positive residual lies in residues $1,3,7,9,15$ modulo $18$,
precisely the classes with no strictly smaller ancestor within two steps.
For the actual annulus,
\[
 \supp Z(A_N)^+\subset(1,64N),\qquad
 \supp J(A_N)\subset(1,96N),\qquad
 \sum_{3\mid x}Z(A_N)(x)\ge21N/4.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The integer potential $\sum_x x\max(\mu(x),0)$ decreases strictly at each
replacement. All arrivals are nonnegative and move downwards, so the
total departure at an active vertex is uniquely determined in descending
order by
$F(x)=\max(\mu(x)+\sum_{q(y)=x}F(y),0)$; at inactive vertices it is zero.
This fixes both the residual and the sum of path currents.
An ancestor smaller than $y$ at distance at most two can use only
valuations $1$ and $2$; checking those four pairs gives exactly the
listed active classes.

Substituting the six annular bands into the three rules gives the
positive-height and current-height bounds. In particular, the first
pass puts positive mass at multiples of $3$ with the following intervals,
residues, moduli and weights:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 (3N,6N;3,12;1),\quad (16N,32N;27,48;1),\\
 (32N,64N;27,48;2),\quad (16N,32N;15,24;1),\quad
 (32N,64N;15,24;2).
 \end{gathered}
\]
Their weighted floor counts sum to $21N/4$: the sum is $42,84,126$
at $N=8,16,24$, and increases by $126$ when $N$ increases by $24$.
The original annulus vanishes at multiples of $3$, and these states
have no predecessors. Subsequent arrivals cannot cancel their positive
mass, so it remains in the terminal residual.
\end{proof}

Consequently any continuation current for this residual with nonpositive
final remainder needs positive current mass at least $21N/4$ at these
vertices. This bounds the \emph{continuation}, not the final summed
annular current: its positive rows can cancel the negative rows of the
normalization. The whole residual retains upper negative atoms below
$144N$. The positive-height bound does not improve the existing
source-scale absorption cutoff $64N$. This is a terminating partial
normalization, not a complete annular descent.

\section{Explicit cancellation after growing delay}

\begin{theorem}[A delayed five-source partial current]\label{thm:release}
Let $h\ge2$, and choose the least $r\ge0$ for which
\[
 \lambda=4(4/3)^h(2/3)^r\le27/8.
\]
Retain $h$ when $\lambda>81/32$. Choose $V\in\{47,155\}$ and impose
\[
 U_0\equiv4^{-h-1}\bigl(3^r2^{-r}(V+1)-2\bigr)
       \pmod{3^{r+4}},\qquad
 X\equiv1+3^hU_0\pmod{3^{h+r+4}},
\]
where the inverses are taken in the displayed ternary modulus. Also require
\[
 2^{\max(h+5,10)}\mid X,\quad X\ge1024,\quad
 X>\frac{c+1/2}{\lambda-81/32},\qquad
 c=\lambda+1-2(2/3)^r.
\]
Put $N=3X/128$, $U=(X-1)/3^h$, and
\[
 v=\frac{2^r(4^{h+1}U+2)}{3^r}-1,\quad
 t=\frac{2v-1}{3},\quad u=\frac{4t-1}{3}.
\]
With $y_j=(3/2)^jX-1$ for $j=1,2,3$, the actual annular submeasure
\[
 \mu=-\delta_u-\delta_v-2\delta_{y_1}
                   +2\delta_{y_2}+2\delta_{y_3}
\]
has an exact finite current $J\le0$ supported in $G_{h+r+1}(N)$.
There are unboundedly many qualifying $h$, and for each one infinitely
many parameter choices on the ladder $N=96\cdot64^k$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Minimality gives $r\ge1$ and $9/4<\lambda\le27/8$. If
$\lambda\le81/32$, replacing $h$ by $h+1$ with the same $r$ gives the
least admissible value $3<(4/3)\lambda\le27/8$. Thus consecutive $h$
cannot both fail the lower bound.

The congruence gives $U\equiv1\pmod3$, so $v_3(X-1)=h$ exactly.
It also makes $v$ an odd integer congruent to $V$ modulo162.
The identity $v=\lambda X-c$ and the size condition give
\[
 81X/32+1/2<v<27X/8-1,\qquad
 y_1<72N<t<y_2<u<v<y_3.
\]
The source coefficients at $y_1,y_2,y_3$ are $(-2,2,2)$.
The residues of $(v,t,u)$ modulo18 are $(11,13,5)$ or
$(11,13,11)$, all in the top band. Their coefficients are therefore
$(-1,0,-1)$. The arrows $u\to t\to v$ have valuations $(2,1)$.

The path from $v$ to $y_1$ has word $(1^r,2^h,3)$. Indeed, reverse
the valuation3 step from $y_1$ to $4X-3$, then the $h$ valuation2 steps:
\[
 w_j=4(4/3)^j(X-1)+1,\quad 0\le j\le h.
\]
These are odd integers. The subsequent reversed valuation1 steps are
$z_l=2^l(w_h+1)/3^l-1$, $0\le l\le r$; the congruence makes each
integral and $z_r=v$. All intermediates other than $v,y_1$ exceed $y_3$:
the $w_j$ increase from $4X-3$, and
$z_{r-1}=(3v+1)/2>y_3$. Thus the indicated sources are distinct and
every valuation is exact.

Writing $L=h+r+1$ and $p_0=v,\ldots,p_L=y_1$ for that path, take
\[
 J=-\delta_u-\delta_t-2\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\delta_{p_j}
                         -4\delta_{y_1}-2\delta_{y_2}.
\]
Telescoping gives $(I-P)J=\mu$. Every coefficient is nonpositive.
The long path starts at the actual source $v$, while $u,y_1,y_2$
independently expose the other arrows, so all rows belong to $G_L(N)$.
This removes four positive units, with $L+4$ current rows and norm
$2L+8$, without an assumption about any subsequent orbit.

The congruences have arbitrarily large solutions by coprimality of their
moduli. They also have power-of2 solutions: cubing
$4^{3^n}=1+3^{n+1}b_n$, with $3\nmid b_n$, proves
$v_3(4^{3^n}-1)=n+1$. Hence 2 has order $2\cdot3^{M-1}$ modulo
$3^M$ and generates all its units. Every required ternary residue is
therefore $2^e$ for an unbounded progression of exponents. As
$X\equiv1\pmod9$, $6\mid e$, and for sufficiently large $e$ the binary
and size requirements hold, with $N=96\cdot64^{e/6-2}$.
\end{proof}

The isolation precision can be sharpened. With $v_3(X-1)\ge h$ and
the binary divisibility required for depth $D$, the same complete-chain
proof works for $D\le h+1$. Indeed, at inverse step $l$, the
\emph{previous} shadow difference $u(X-1)$ has ternary valuation
\[
 h+k-(l-1)\ge h-D+2\ge1.
\]
This is exactly what is needed to transfer admissibility to the next
integral auxiliary state. At the final step its difference need only
be integral. Before the first integral auxiliary state, the old binary
argument is unchanged. The coefficient invariant and both endpoint
closure arguments therefore apply without alteration.
For the preceding family the original sources consequently form a
complete mass2 component through depth $h+1$. Also $r\le h$, since
$4(8/9)^h\le256/81<27/8$ for $h\ge2$, so $L\le2h+1$.
Thus the construction resolves a designated block after arbitrarily
long initial isolation, with a linear exposure bound in $h$. It is an exact
\emph{submeasure} certificate, not a claim that the whole component
at depth $L$ has no additional positive sources.

On $N=96\cdot64^k$, take $X=2^{6(k+2)}$.
The elementary valuation formula gives
$h=v_3(X-1)=2+v_3(k+2)$, and its binary exponent exceeds $h+5$.
The least sufficient exposure depth, allowing infinity, therefore obeys
the stronger bound
\[
 d_*(96\cdot64^k)\ge v_3(k+2)+4.
\]

For $h=r=2$ the ladder indices include $k\equiv89,575\pmod{729}$.
A complete finite example at $N=340131840$ changes from mass2 at

At $N=60555264$, by contrast, the first new attachment at depth9

\section{A partial extension from retained input arrows}

\begin{theorem}[Retained-graph extension]\label{thm:retained}
For $8\mid N$, let $H$ be a supplied finite exact current for
$I_N=2U_N-U_{N/4}-U_{3N/4}$. Retain every arrow in $H$, the explicit
$K_N$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:localinput}, and the fixed-core current $E_N$,
even when their coefficients cancel in a sum. This graph absorbs every
odd seed at most $N$ and a specified subset of $(N,3N/2)$.
It yields a packet current
\[
 \PiR_N=(I-P)J_{\rm imp}-R_N,\qquad R_N\ge0,
 \qquad |R_N|=25N/288+O(1),
\]
without exposing any new arrow. The guaranteed absorbed outer subset
has size $47N/288+O(1)$, a fraction tending to $47/72$ of that shell.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $B=3N/2$. The identity
\[
 K_N=3P(U_N-U_{N/4})+3(U_N-U_{N/4})-U_{3N/2}-U_{3N/4}
\]
shows that its support is exactly the odd states $1<x<B$:
its coefficient is $-2$ modulo3 below $3N/4$, and $-1$ modulo3 above.
All summands are supported below $B$.

The exact current for the nonnegative inner source supplies paths to1
from each inner seed. For $y\le N/4$, iterate $F(x)=4x+1$ to the first
$z>N/4$. Then $z\le N-3$ and $S(z)=S(y)$. The row at $y$ and the
supplied suffix from $S(z)$ give a path to1 from $y$. Thus every seed
through $N$ is absorbed on the retained graph.

Here is the full list of components of the $K_N$-only graph missing
every state at most $N$, including1. They have root $N<x<B$ and
boundary successor $s=(3x+1)/2>B$:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 x\equiv3\pmod{12}&\{x,s\}\\
 x\equiv7\pmod{12},\ x>9N/8&\{x,s\}\\
 x\equiv7,19\pmod{36},\ x<9N/8&
   \{(4x-1)/3,x,s\}.
 \end{array}
\]
To check completeness, an outer state $y\equiv5\pmod8$ has a smaller
odd sibling $(y-1)/4<N$, and $y\equiv2\pmod3$ has predecessor
$(2y-1)/3<N$. The remaining states have valuation1 or2.
Valuation1 gives the roots in the table. At valuation2 the successor
is below $9N/8$; if it has valuation at least2, the next state is at
most $(9y+7)/16<N$. Otherwise it is a root in the table.
Such a root has a predecessor below $B$ only when $x<9N/8$.
That predecessor $p=(4x-1)/3$ has a further predecessor below $B$
only when $x\equiv31\pmod{36}$, in which case
$(2p-1)/3=(8x-5)/9<N$. All other predecessors are iterated
$F$-lifts and exceed $B$. This proves the list.

Give $R_N$ weight1 at each two-vertex component root and weight2 at
each three-vertex component root. Let $Q_N$ put weight1 at the
extra predecessors in the latter case. Then
$W_{\rm bad}=(I-P)Q_N+R_N$ counts each uncovered outer seed once,
and $W_{\rm good}=U_{3N/2}-U_N-W_{\rm bad}\ge0$ is absorbed.
Extract on the supplied graph an exact current $T_N$ for
$2U_{N/4}+W_{\rm good}$. The automatic input identity gives
\[
 J_{\rm imp}=H-K_N-E_N-T_N-Q_N,\qquad
 \PiR_N=(I-P)J_{\rm imp}-R_N.
\]
Source multiplicities and every residual sign are explicit.

Counting the displayed residue intervals gives
$|R_{N+288}|=|R_N|+25$. The 36 initial phases give
$-19/9\le |R_N|-25N/288\le5/4$. Since the outer shell contains
$N/4$ odd seeds, the guaranteed absorbed count is
$N/4-|R_N|=47N/288+O(1)$.
\end{proof}

The residual vanishes at $N=8$ and is nonzero at every allowed
$N\ge16$. On $N=96\cdot64^k$ its mass is exactly $25N/288-4/3$.
The construction is conditional on the supplied inner current.
It does not prove a repeatable contraction of $R_N$. Further
connections in the supplied paths may improve it, but are additional
graph information.

\section{Signed path collapse and retained frontiers}

Let $E_0$ contain the valuation1 arrows and those valuation2 arrows
whose source is2 modulo3. Its unique incoming predecessor, if present, is
\[
 p(y)=
 \begin{cases}
 (2y-1)/3,&y\equiv2\pmod3,\\
 (4y-1)/3,&y\equiv13\pmod{18}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Every vertex has in-degree and out-degree at most1. Two successive
valuation2 arrows are impossible: the first ends at residue1 modulo3,
which cannot start a second allowed valuation2 arrow.
Every two backward steps therefore reduce the state by a factor
strictly below $8/9$. Thus every vertex has a unique finite backward
root, in the inactive classes $\{1,3,7,9,15\}$ modulo18.
This proves no general statement about its forward path ending.

\begin{theorem}[Two finite path constructions]\label{thm:pathcollapse}
For any finite integer source $\mu$, there is a finite linear signed
collapse $B\mu$ to its $E_0$ roots with
$\mu=(I-P)J_B+B\mu$. It is idempotent. There is also a finite
normalization with $J_{\rm cap}\le0$ and positive residual only at roots.
On a path $x_0,\ldots,x_m$ rooted at $x_0$, with coefficients
$a_i=\mu(x_i)$ and zeros inserted, its root coefficient is
\[
 \max_{0\le j\le m}\sum_{i=0}^j a_i.
\]
This is the minimum root residual among nonpositive $E_0$ currents
whose residual away from that root is nonpositive.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take the finite union of all source ancestries. For the linear collapse,
move each signed coefficient $c$ at $y$ back to its root with current
$-c$ on every preceding path edge. Telescoping gives the identity.
Its root coefficient is $\sum_i a_i$, and
$J_B(x_i)=-\sum_{j>i}a_j$.

For the capacity construction, put $c_{m+1}=0$ and process backwards:
\[
 c_i=\max(a_i+c_{i+1},0),\quad
 J_{\rm cap}(x_{i-1})=-c_i,\quad
 Z_{\rm cap}(x_i)=a_i+c_{i+1}-c_i.
\]
The root coefficient $a_0+c_1$ is the maximum displayed prefix sum.
All nonroot residuals are nonpositive. For any competitor with
nonpositive current, summing its divergence on a prefix gives
$\sum_{i\le j}Z(x_i)=\sum_{i\le j}a_i-J(x_j)$.
Since its other residuals and current are nonpositive, its root
coefficient is at least every prefix sum. The constructed current
attains this lower bound. The old two-edge shortcut normalizer is
one such competitor, so retaining intermediate negative capacity
cannot increase its root residual.
\end{proof}

The linear signed current need not be nonpositive. At $N=96$ the old
normalizer leaves positive mass1859, the capacity version1414, and
signed collapse1337. These finite values are not universal contraction
constants. A literal positive-forward/old-normalize iteration stabilizes
but retains at least $N$ positive mass on every annulus; its exact trap
classes and floor counts are proved in
\texttt{alternating/ALTERNATING\_PATHS.md}.

Keeping a component's furthest exposed source $x_m$ instead of only its
root gives another exact table:
\[
 J_F(x_i)=\sum_{j=0}^{i}a_j\quad(0\le i<m),\qquad
 F\mu=\Bigl(\sum_j a_j\Bigr)\delta_{x_m}.
\]
The current is zero elsewhere. This retains the path and the frontier
needed for further exposure.
Pushing a root across an $E_0$ arrow and immediately collapsing it
simply returns to that root.

An explicit example shows the limit of height descent. For
$N=96\cdot64^k$, put $L=12+6k$ and
$x_j=3^j2^{L-j}-1$, $0\le j<L$.
These form a complete $E_0$ component rooted at $2^L-1$, with exactly
the source weights $(-2,2,2)$ at $x_1,x_2,x_3$.
All preceding arrows have valuation1; the last has valuation
$3+v_2(L)\ge4$ and exits $E_0$ to
$w=(3^L-1)/2^{2+v_2(L)}$. Telescoping gives
\[
 -2\delta_{x_1}+2\delta_{x_2}+2\delta_{x_3}
 =(I-P)\left(-2\delta_{x_1}
       +2\sum_{j=3}^{L-1}\delta_{x_j}\right)+2\delta_w.
\]
For odd $k$, $w=(3^L-1)/8\equiv1\pmod{18}$ is already inactive,
and $w/(2^L-1)\to\infty$. The formula crosses a known component
exit with growing depth, but retains positive mass at a potentially
much larger root. A further cancellation or decreasing parameter
is still needed.

\section{Phase coverage does not follow from the delay order}

\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}

 \text{exceptional phase}\\ \hline
2&5&2^{10}\mid X&X\ge497664&U\equiv16\pmod{27}\\
3&7&2^{11}\mid X&X\ge8957952&U\equiv31\pmod{81}

\section{From annular cancellation to smaller-seed descent}
This section gives a conditional interface to the outer profile $R_N$.
Fix $8\mid M$, set $N=64M$, and supply a finite exact current $H$ for
$U_N$. Every positive source $y$ of $A_M$ has an ancestor at most $N$
within two arrows. For $y\le N$ this is immediate. For
$N<y<9N/8$, residues $11,17$ modulo $18$ use $(2y-1)/3$; residue $13$
uses
\[
(8y-5)/9\longrightarrow(4y-1)/3\longrightarrow y.
\]
For $9N/8<y<9N/4$, the only positive residue is $17$, and the path is
\[
(4y-5)/9\longrightarrow(2y-1)/3\longrightarrow y.
\]
The initial states lie below $N$, and all displayed valuations are exact.
The graph of $H$ therefore contains the absorption paths of these positive
sources.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional annular-to-complement transfer]
Suppose a supplied finite exact block satisfies
\[
\nu^+-\nu^-=(I-P)J,\qquad
0\le\nu^+\le A_M^+,\quad 0\le\nu^-\le A_M^-,
\]
with disjoint positive and negative supports. Known absorption through
$N$ supplies an exact current $H_+$ for $\nu^+$ on its existing graph,
and hence
\[
\nu^-=(I-P)(H_+-J).
\]
Every residual root $x$ of $R_N$ whose exit
$t=(3x+1)/2$ lies in $\supp\nu^-$ can be absorbed with its full
coefficient, either one or two.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The ancestor formulas give $H_+$. A finite exact signed current with a
nonnegative source forces every source into the component containing
the absorber: summing divergence over any other finite component gives
zero, contradicting positive source mass there. On a functional graph,
connection to the absorber supplies a directed path to it. Unrelated
zero-source cycle components do not change this argument.

Each $R_N$ root is $3$ or $7$ modulo $12$. Its valuation-one exit lies
in $(3N/2,9N/4)$, is $5$ or $11$ modulo $18$, and has coefficient
$A_M(t)=-1$. If $t$ is certified by the displayed identity, append
$x\to t$. A proved path may be multiplied by two when $R_N(x)=2$;
this does not spend the original annular coefficient twice.
\end{proof}
Writing $R_N=(I-P)T+R_{\rm rem}$ gives the upgraded identity
\[
\PiR_N=(I-P)(J_{\rm old}-T)-R_{\rm rem},
\qquad R_{\rm rem}\ge0.
\]
Every uncovered coefficient remains explicit.

The old packed five-source trees cover precisely the roots
$9183+768j$ for their admissible indices $j=9s$ or $9s+3$.
Their number is $C_{\rm old}(M)=N/9216+O(1)$.
The other existing short families have exits below the relevant range
or certify roots already removed by the retained-graph construction.
Thus cancelled annular mass and coverage of $R_N$ are distinct quantities.

The delayed family supplies a more demanding branch. Using its
$h,r,X,v$ at cutoff $M=3X/128$, put $N=3X/2$ and $t=(2v-1)/3$.
Then
\[
9N/8<t<3N/2,\quad t\equiv31\pmod{36},\quad R_N(t)=1,
\]
and its exact first descent to the known interval is
\[
t\longrightarrow N-1,\qquad
\text{valuation word }1^{r+1},2^h,3.
\]
The path has $h+r+2\le2h+2$ arrows, every state before its endpoint
exceeds $N$, and its peak exceeds $(4/3)^hN$. This gives infinitely
many explicit shrinking branches with unbounded excursions, including
branches on the required ladder. It assumes no absorption above $N$.
A companion example has $h=r=3$, $X=24315904$, $N=36473856$,
and residual coefficient two at $40480795$; its ten-arrow path ends
at $N-1$. Its current has coefficient two on every origin.
These branches and the transfer do not cover all of $R_N$.

\section{Sharp bounded source clusters in the restricted graph}
Let $N\ge8$ with $8\mid N$. Keep the graph $E_0$ of valuation-one edges and valuation-two edges
with source $2$ modulo $3$. A source cluster comprises all nonzero
$A_N$ sources in one complete $E_0$ component; the unrestricted
exposure graph is different. Put $Q=9N/4$ and define
\[
\widehat h(x)=
\begin{cases}x,&x\equiv1\pmod3,\\x/2,&x\equiv2\pmod3.\end{cases}
\]
The empty half-bands in the annular table give
$Q<\widehat h(x)<32Q$ at every nonzero source.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp source-span bound]
Two annular sources on one $E_0$ path have even separation at most
$58$ or odd separation at most $63$. The bound $63$ is attained.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An allowed step from type one multiplies adjusted height by more than
$3/4$; from type two it multiplies it by more than $3/2$.
Type-one steps cannot be consecutive. A segment of length $2m$ thus
has ratio greater than $(9/8)^m$, and one of length $2m+1$ has ratio
greater than $(3/4)(9/8)^m$. The source endpoints have ratio below $32$,
while
\[
(9/8)^{30}>32,\qquad
(3/4)(9/8)^{32}>32.
\]
For sharpness take $x=3\cdot2^{95}t-5$, $t\ge1$,
$Q=18\lfloor x/18\rfloor$, and $N=8\lfloor x/18\rfloor$.
The exact word $(1,2)^{31},1$ ends at
$2\cdot3^{64}t-7$. Its endpoints are nonzero sources in bands zero
and five. Total valuation is $94$; the exact cylinder includes the
extra bit and has modulus $2^{95}$. The bound already proved excludes
any other source outside this segment.
\end{proof}
At most $63$ predecessor and $63$ successor queries from a source
therefore recover its entire cluster. If its retained segment is
$x_0,\ldots,x_d$, with $a_i=A_N(x_i)$, put
\[
J(x_i)=\sum_{j=0}^i a_j\quad(0\le i<d),\qquad J=0
\text{ elsewhere}.
\]
Its residual is $(\sum_i a_i)\delta_{x_d}$. Summing these disjoint
source clusters yields exact finite preprocessing, with current rows
below $192N$ and positive cost at most
$63\|A_N^+\|_1=6237N/4$. Every positive frontier is retained.
A sufficient period for all bounded forward and backward guards is
$2^{96}3^{65}$; the full arithmetic description is in
\texttt{window\_components/WINDOW\_COMPONENTS.md}.

These bounds do not control the future exit distance. If a forbidden
edge $x\to y$ has valuation at least three, the root of the target
component (with root$(1)=1$) is below $x/4$; the comparison is with the originating exit
vertex, which may be far above the last source. Valuation-two forbidden
edges instead land directly at a smaller inactive root.
For the Mersenne family with odd $k$, the first exit is already a root
$w=(3^L-1)/8>144N$, and its entire target component has no original
annular source. The transported positive mass is then an auxiliary
source there. A recursion must handle such components and prove eventual
progress; bounded source preprocessing does not establish termination.

\section{The ternary order alone cannot bound the depth}
\begin{theorem}[Arbitrary depth at fixed ternary order]\label{thm:fixedh}
For every $D\ge1$, suppose
\[
2^{\max(D+4,10)}\mid X,\qquad X\equiv10\pmod{3^{D+2}},
\qquad N=3X/128.
\]
Then $8\mid N$ and $v_3(X-1)=2$. The complete component of $144N-1$
in $G_D(N)$ is
\[
y_1\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow y_{D+3},
\qquad y_j=3^jX/2^j-1.
\]
Its only source coefficients are $-2,+2,+2$ at $y_1,y_2,y_3$.
It has mass two and excludes a one-sided current supported on $G_D(N)$.
For every $D$ there are infinitely many examples on $N=96\cdot64^k$.
Consequently no finite-valued bound depending only on $h=v_3(X-1)$
can bound sufficient exposure depth.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We give the incoming-history argument and specify its finite arithmetic
certificate. Write
\[
T_-(c)=(3c-1)/2^{v_2(3c-1)}
\]
on positive odd integers. The following finite barrier is used:
if $u_0=2^s3^r\ge4$, $r,s\ge0$, $1<c_0<u_0$ is odd, and
$T_-^\ell(c_0)=1$ for some finite $\ell$, then every positive odd
Syracuse ancestor of $z_0=10u_0-c_0$ is at least $35$.
Indeed the orbits of the odd integers through $33$ have exactly $54$
states, with maximum $3077$. A counterexample would satisfy
$9u_0<z_0<10u_0$ within this finite set. Exhausting all $2,3$-smooth
$u_0$ in those intervals gives $22$ pairs. Six have $c_0=1$.
The remaining twelve distinct offsets all enter either
\[
5\to7\to5
\quad\hbox{or}\quad
17\to25\to37\to55\to41\to61\to91\to17
\]
under $T_-$, contradicting the assumed hit at $1$.
The full $54$-state set, $22$-row table, and connecting paths are
listed and checked in
\texttt{phase\_depth/FIXED\_H\_DEPTH.md} and its adjacent checker.
This is a bounded exhaustive certificate, not an asymptotic inference.

Remove any initial on-chain part of an inverse history from $y_k$.
Its first departing valuation is odd and at least three.
At inverse length $\ell\le D$, with valuation sum $A$, write
\[
x=uX-c,\qquad u=3^{k-\ell}2^{A-k},\qquad c>0.
\]
An inverse step of valuation $a$ updates
$u'=2^a u/3$, $c'=(2^ac+1)/3$.
Before the first crossing $A\ge k$, actual admissibility makes $c$
a positive odd integer, including at the crossing. Comparing the
same inverse maps at $X=1$ with the all-valuation-one history gives
$c<u$ through that crossing: its length is at most $k$, the comparison
value $(3/2)^{k-\ell}-1$ is nonnegative, and the first departure
strictly raises it. Before the crossing,
\[
u=3^{A-\ell}(3/2)^{k-A}\ge9,\qquad x>9(X-1)>144N-1.
\]
At the crossing $u_0=3^{k-\ell}2^{A-k}\ge4$ and
$1<c_0<u_0$. Reversing the offset updates gives
$T_-^\ell(c_0)=1$, since $3c'-1=2^ac$ with $c$ odd.
The finite barrier applies.

After the crossing set $z=10u-c$. The difference
$x-z=u(X-10)$ is an even integer divisible by $3$ throughout every
history of length at most $D$: its ternary valuation is at least
$D+2+k-\ell\ge3$, and $u$ has no binary denominator.
Thus the actual inverse word also defines a positive odd inverse
word from $z_0$. The barrier gives $z\ge35$ and
\[
x=u(X-10)+z>\frac{zX}{10}\ge\frac72X>\frac{27}{8}X-1.
\]
Every off-chain incoming source is excluded. The bottom predecessor
$X-1$ is a multiple of three, has zero source coefficient, and has
no odd predecessor. The top vertex has no exposed outgoing edge,
by the same forward height bound as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:unboundeddepth}. The displayed chain is therefore
the entire component.

Finally, the elementary lifting order of $2$ modulo $3^{D+2}$ is
$2\cdot3^{D+1}$. There is an exponent $e$ with
$2^e\equiv10\pmod{3^{D+2}}$; reduction modulo nine forces $6\mid e$.
Adding multiples of the order makes $e\ge\max(D+4,12)$.
Then $X=2^e$ gives $N=96\cdot64^{e/6-2}$, and infinitely many
such exponents remain in that class.
\end{proof}
The offsets that actually occur reach $1$ under $T_-$. The nontrivial
cycles above exclude hypothetical small shadows; they do not trap the
actual offsets. Also, $X$ changes with $D$. No fixed integer is proved
to remain isolated forever.

\section{A complete half-size coalescence family}
\begin{theorem}[Conditional halving of the outer residual]\label{thm:halving}
Write an odd $x\equiv3\pmod4$ uniquely as $x=2^at-1$ with
$a\ge2$ and $t$ odd. If $t\equiv3^a\pmod4$, then
\[
S^a(x)=S^a(q),\qquad q=(x-1)/2.
\]
For $8\mid N$, let $G_N$ be the full-weight restriction of $R_N$
to this family. A supplied exact current for $U_N$ gives a finite
exact current $T$ for $G_N$ using only its existing absorption paths
and the first $a$ arrows from each selected root. The remaining profile
$R'_N=R_N-G_N$ satisfies
\[
|R'_N|=\frac{25N}{576}+O(\log N),\qquad
\left||R'_N|-\frac{25N}{576}\right|
\le6\left\lfloor\log_2(3N/2)\right\rfloor.
\]
It consists precisely of the roots with $t\equiv-3^a\pmod4$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first $a-1$ valuations of $x$ and first $a-2$ valuations of $q$
are one. With $z=3^{a-1}t\equiv3\pmod4$, their next states are
\[
S^{a-1}(q)=(z-1)/2,\qquad
S^{a-1}(x)=2z-1=4S^{a-1}(q)+1.
\]
Now $S(4v+1)=S(v)$ proves the merger. The difference
\[
D_x=\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}\delta_{S^j(x)}
       -\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}\delta_{S^j(q)}
\]
has divergence $\delta_x-\delta_q$, with rows and mass at $1$ killed.
Every selected residual root lies below $3N/2$, so $q<3N/4<N$.
Add the supplied absorption current for $q$, multiplied by the
coefficient $R_N(x)$, to this bridge. Summing certifies all of $G_N$.
A nonnegative current can be extracted on the same finite graph by
the nonnegative-source argument used in the annular transfer.
No unknown larger absorption input is used. In particular
\[
\PiR_N=(I-P)(J_{\rm old}-T)-R'_N.
\]

For the count, the selected class for each $a$ has residue
$b_a=2^a(1\text{ if }a\text{ is even, else }3)-1$
modulo $p_a=2^{a+2}$. It has valuation $v_2(x+1)=a$ exactly.
Intersect each of the four weighted R17 progressions with this class
by the Chinese remainder theorem. For a progression modulo
$m\in\{12,36\}$ the intersection has period $p_a(m/4)$.
Its count on a strict interval differs from interval length divided
by this period by at most one. The weighted widths divided by $m/4$
sum to $25N/72$, while the four weights sum to six.
Writing $A=\lfloor\log_2(3N/2)\rfloor$, there are no roots with
$a>A$, and
\[
\sum_{a=2}^A2^{-a-2}=\frac18-2^{-A-2}.
\]
Hence
\[
\left||G_N|-\frac{25N}{576}\right|
\le6(A-1)+\frac{25}{216}.
\]
Combining this with the earlier exact bounds on
$|R_N|-25N/288$ gives the stated $6A$ bound.
The exact CRT floor formula is recorded in
\texttt{residual\_return/SHRINKING\_COALESCENCE.md}.
\end{proof}

This family includes arbitrarily long excursions on a required ray:
for even $a\ge8$, take $x=2^a-1$ and $N=12\cdot2^{a-4}$.
Then $R_N(x)=1$, and $S^{a-1}(x)=2\cdot3^{a-1}-1$ has
unbounded ratio to $N$. The bridge has a prescribed finite endpoint
even though the common state need not be below $N$.

The complementary branch is explicit but not resolved. Put
\[
d=v_2(3^{a-1}t-1)\ge2,\qquad
w=(3^{a-1}t-1)/2^d.
\]
Then
\[
S^{a-1}(q)=w,\qquad S^a(x)=3\cdot2^{d-1}w+1.
\]
For $a\ge3$, $q$ itself belongs to the selected family. This fact
does not connect $x$ to it. For example $x=27$ is an actual residual
root at $N=24$, but $S^2(27)=31$ and $S^2(13)=1$.
The half-mass improvement cannot be iterated without a new closure theorem.

\section{The residual target admits a noncircular sparse induction}
\begin{corollary}[Conditional completion on interleaved rays]\label{cor:residualrays}
Suppose that at every $N=a2^b$ with

Then Collatz holds.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The retained-graph construction and Theorem~\ref{thm:halving},
together with the assumed rule, extend $U_N$ to $U_{3N/2}$.
In increasing order the five rays have consecutive ratios
\[
7/6,\quad8/7,\quad9/8,\quad7/6,\quad8/7,
\]
repeated on doubling the scale. Each next cutoff is below $3N/2$,
so extracting its paths supplies its input current. The finite base

\section{Complete elementary bridge classes and the induced map}
Let $F(x)=4x+1$ and let $\mathcal B$ consist of positive odd integers
$q\equiv1\pmod8$ together with complementary climbers
$q=2^at-1$, $a\ge2$, $t\equiv-3^a\pmod4$.
\begin{theorem}[Complete elementary normal form]\label{thm:bridgenormal}
The equivalence generated by the identities $S(F(x))=S(x)$ and
Theorem~\ref{thm:halving} has exactly the components
\[
\mathcal C_q=\{F^k(q),F^k(2q+1):k\ge0\},\qquad q\in\mathcal B.
\]
Their unique minima define a terminating normalization $\nu(x)$.
There is an explicit finite integer current $D_x$ with
$\operatorname{div}D_x=\delta_x-\delta_{\nu(x)}$.
Moreover $\nu S=\nu S\nu$, so $T(q)=\nu(S(q))$ satisfies
$T^n\nu=\nu S^n$ for every $n\ge0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inverse $F$ steps are available exactly at $x\equiv5\pmod8$ and
strictly decrease. Their terminal root is either $1\pmod8$ or $3\pmod4$.
Every selected root $p=2^at-1$ has a canonical partner $(p-1)/2$:
it is $1\pmod8$ for $a=2$, and complementary for $a\ge3$.
Conversely each canonical $q$ has exactly the selected partner $2q+1$.
These edges pair two distinct $F$-rays and exhaust all elementary edges.
Strip the inverse $F$ steps, then halve once if selected. Equal successors
give the current $\delta_x-\delta_r$ to its $F$-root $r$; append the
halving prefix difference when necessary. This proves the finite bridge.

For $q\equiv1\pmod8$,
$S(2q+1)=F(S(q))$. For complementary $q$, $S(q)$ is canonical and
$S(2q+1)=2S(q)+1$. Thus successors of both bases normalize equally;
$F$-related points already have equal successors. This proves the
semiconjugacy. The class of1 absorbs within two actual steps, so
termination of $T$ for every canonical seed is equivalent to Collatz.
\end{proof}
In particular, no sequence using only these elementary identities lowers
an already canonical root. This does not exclude new bridges through
additional arrows. The full inverse description is two rays under
$q\mapsto64q+49$, with the following first points and optional singleton:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
y\bmod3&\text{singleton}&\text{ray bases}\\\hline
0&\text{none}&(8y+3)/3,\ (16y+3)/3\\
1&\text{none}&(4y-1)/3,\ (32y+19)/3\\
2&(2y-1)/3&(64y+19)/3,\ (128y+83)/3
\end{array}
\]
They follow by requiring $S(q)\in\mathcal C_y$ for $q\equiv1\pmod8$,
or using the valuation1 predecessor. Greedy inversion is insufficient:
for $y=57+72j$, its depth2 choice is $409+512j$, whereas the true minimum
is $203+256j$. Complete proofs, exact CRT counts and finite inverse bounds
are in the normal-form note under
\path{experiments/residual_progress/bridge_normal_form/}.

\section{Complete complementary run bridges and their remaining mass}
For a complementary root $x=2^at-1$, put
\[
d=v_2(3^{a-1}t-1)\ge2,\qquad w=(3^{a-1}t-1)/2^d.
\]
\begin{theorem}[Normalized run construction]\label{thm:runbridge}
Define
\[
f(x)=\begin{cases}
3^{(d-1)/2}w,&d\text{ odd},\\
2\cdot3^{d/2}w+1,&d\text{ even},
\end{cases}
\qquad f_*(x)=\nu(f(x)).
\]
Every such root has an explicit finite bridge with divergence
$\delta_x-\delta_{f_*(x)}$. Given an exact $U_N$ current, all atoms
of $R'_N$ with $f_*(x)\le N$ can therefore be certified with their
full multiplicities. If this removed profile is $V^*_N$, then
the preceding identity $\PiR_N=\operatorname{div}J-R'_N$ becomes
\[
\PiR_N=\operatorname{div}(J-T_N)-(R'_N-V^*_N),
\quad \operatorname{div}T_N=V^*_N.
\]
There is an explicit convergent series $\beta_{\rm run}$ such that
\[
|V^*_N|=\beta_{\rm run}N+O(\log^4N),\qquad
\beta_{\rm run}=0.02399251818395744\ldots,
\]
uniformly for $8\mid N$, $N\ge8$. The remaining leading coefficient is
$25/576-\beta_{\rm run}=0.01941025959382034\ldots$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first run gives $S^a(x)=3\cdot2^{d-1}w+1$.
For odd $C,w$ and $e\ge3$,
\[
S(C2^ew+1)=3C2^{e-2}w+1
\]
has valuation exactly2. If $d$ is odd, this reaches $F(f(x))$ and
one further arrow coalesces with $S(f(x))$. If $d$ is even, it reaches
$f(x)$ itself. Subtract the one target arrow in the odd case; append
the finite normalization bridge in both cases. Adding the supplied
currents of the canonical targets gives divergence $V^*_N$.
Finite graph extraction then produces a nonnegative absorption current,
retaining signed intermediate rows until extraction.

For the count, the disjoint exact cylinders are
\[
t\equiv3^{1-a}(1+2^d)\pmod{2^{d+1}},\qquad
x\pmod{2^{a+d+1}}.
\]
Intersect these with the weighted residual bands and the canonical
strata from Theorem~\ref{thm:bridgenormal}. Let $L_{a,d}$ be the
leading slope of $f$:
\[
L_{a,d}=\frac{3^{a-1+\lfloor d/2\rfloor}}{2^{a+d}}
\begin{cases}1,&d\text{ odd},\\2,&d\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
Set
\[
g(h)=\frac79\max\{0,\min(9/8,h)-1\}
+\frac23\max\{0,\min(3/2,h)-9/8\}.
\]
Then $\beta_{\rm run}=\sum_{a,d\ge2}2^{-a-d-1}h_{a,d}$, where
\[
h_{a,d}=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\sum_{k\ge0}4^{-k}
 \left[\tfrac12g(4^k/L_{a,d})+\tfrac14g(2\cdot4^k/L_{a,d})\right],
 &d\text{ odd},\\
\tfrac12[g(1/L_{a,d})+g(2/L_{a,d})],&d\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
The inner sum has an exact geometric tail once both widths saturate.
The tail beyond $a+d>K$ is at most $25(K-1)/(72\cdot2^{K+1})$.
Canonical counting in an odd progression has error $O(\log N)$,
uniform in its modulus. Only $O(\log^3N)$ run/normalization strata
can contribute; their affine endpoint offsets are uniformly bounded
whenever they intersect the target interval. Summing these errors and
the bounded omitted tails gives $O(\log^4N)$. The exact CRT formula,
offset bounds and rational enclosure are in
\path{experiments/residual_progress/complementary_bridge/RUN_BLOCK_BRIDGES.md}.
\end{proof}
At $N=6144$ the rule certifies146 of269 remaining weighted units.
It is not a repeatable contraction. For $a\equiv7\pmod{16}$,
$x=2^a-1$ has $d=3$ and an already canonical return
$f_*(x)=(3^a-3)/8$, with unbounded ratio to $x$.
Also $27\mapsto31\mapsto91$ raises height twice and sends the labels
$(a,d)$ from $(2,2)$ to $(5,4)$ and back. Thus those labels alone
cannot carry a strictly decreasing rank for every return.

\section{Infinitely many known targets at every height}
Let $\mathcal I_N=\{x>0\text{ odd}:\nu(x)\le N\}$.
This notation denotes a set, distinct from the earlier inner source $I_N$.
\begin{theorem}[Uniform high-band count]\label{thm:knownfibres}
For integers $X\ge N\ge8$,
\[
\#(\mathcal I_N\cap[X,2X))=\frac N4+O(\log N),
\]
with an absolute constant independent of $X$. A supplied exact $U_N$
current certifies each individual member of $\mathcal I_N$ by a finite
explicit bridge with $O(\log N)$ extra rows, independently of its height.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write the two rays uniformly as $2^sq+b_s$, $q\in\mathcal B$, where
$b_s=(2^s-1)/3$ for even $s$ and $(2^{s+1}-1)/3$ for odd $s$.
Let $K_N(u)$ count canonical integers at most $\min(N,u)$, and put
\[
A_s(t)=K_N((t-1-b_s)/2^s).
\]
The exact band count telescopes to
\[
K_N(N)+\sum_{s\ge1}\bigl[A_s(2X)-A_{s-1}(X)\bigr].
\]
The arguments in each difference differ by at most $1/2$, so each
integer count differs by at most1. A nonzero correction requires
$2X/(N+1)<2^s<2X$, leaving $O(\log N)$ corrections uniformly in $X$.
The disjoint canonical binary classes give $K_N(N)=N/4+O(\log N)$.
For certification use Theorem~\ref{thm:bridgenormal} and the supplied
path of $q\le N$. Entire $F$-rays share a successor; the one possible
selected partner is $2q+1\le2N+1$, with halving length $O(\log N)$.
This proves the row bound, without claiming a bound on integer bit cost.
\end{proof}
At $X=HN$, the density among odd integers is

known forward paths. No single finite current certifies the infinite union
at once, and spatial density alone gives no guarantee that an unresolved

\section{Two exact rates for specified slope-prefix rules}
The independent lane's D/M/H recognizers test slopes
$\lambda_j=3^j/2^{A_j}$ at completed valuation prefixes, conditional
on initial valuation1. D accepts when $(3/2)\lambda_j<1$;
M also accepts an odd valuation at least3 when $\lambda_j<1$;
H also accepts a run of1s closed by a valuation at least3 when its
origin $r$ has $(3/2)\lambda_r<2$.
For exactly these symbolic rules the undecided cylinder measures satisfy
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}p_n^{1/n}=\rho
=\frac{c^c}{2^c(c-1)^{c-1}}=0.9465045768\ldots,
\qquad c=\log_2 3,
\]
per odd step, and
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}u_m^{1/m}=\beta_{\rm bit}
=\rho^{1/c}=0.9659065532\ldots
\]
per binary bit. The second clock retains the unfinished next valuation.
Exponential-moment bounds give the upper estimates. Rotating positive
compositions with total $\lfloor cn\rfloor$ gives the lower odd-step
estimate; compositions of a fixed total bit budget give the separate bit
estimate. The fixed escape prefix $(1,1,1,2)$, represented by
$x\equiv47\pmod{64}$, raises the slope above2 and closes all low-origin
runs. Appended zero-barrier compositions then avoid all three rules,
giving matching lower rates. The complete elementary argument is in
\path{experiments/residual_progress/cylinder_barrier/CYLINDER_RATES.md}.

The external exact-closing H implementation misses an unfinished
valuation already known to be at least3. At four bits,
$x\equiv3\pmod{16}$ is selected; using that fact changes the conditional
undecided measure from1 to$3/4$. Either convention has the same limiting
rate. Equal exponential rates need not imply constant-factor ratios.
Nor do these symbolic results give actual interval counts with a growing
bit budget: affine constants and increasing-modulus discrepancies need
separate control. They do not rule out arbitrary bridges to
$\mathcal I_N$, whose membership is explicitly arithmetic.

\section{Saturating all supplied paths into a finite ordinary graph}
Let $Y_N$ be the union of the actual absorbing paths supplied by a finite
exact $U_N$ current, including1, and set $Q_N=\nu(Y_N)$.
The exact closure under the elementary identities and forward $S$ arrows is
\[
\mathcal E_N=\nu^{-1}(Q_N),\qquad T(Q_N)\subseteq Q_N.
\]
Indeed elementary identities preserve $\nu$, and taking a successor
advances its value by $T$. Conversely $S$ followed by normalization
generates every quotient successor, then inverse elementary identities
generate its whole fibre. The supplied paths make $Q_N$ finite and absorbing.

\begin{theorem}[A finite core for the infinite saturation]\label{thm:saturation}
For every nonempty finite canonical set $Q$ with $T(Q)\subseteq Q$, let
\[
W_Q=\{S(q),3q+2:q\in Q\}.
\]
Then $S(\nu^{-1}(Q))=W_Q$, $S(W_Q)\subseteq W_Q$,
$|W_Q|=2|Q|$, and $\max W_Q=3\max Q+2$.
If $Q$ absorbs under $T$, its finite ordinary $S$ table certifies every
member of $\nu^{-1}(Q)$ in at most $2|Q|$ arrows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The two rays in each canonical class have constant successors $S(q)$
and $S(2q+1)=3q+2$, and these normalize to $T(q)\in Q$.
Their $2|Q|$ bases are distinct $F$-roots; equality of two successors
would place those bases on one $F$-ray, a contradiction.
This proves the identities and closure. The semiconjugacy proves absorption
of the finite table from absorption of $Q$. One initial arrow enters
$W_Q$, followed by at most $2|Q|-1$ arrows to1.
\end{proof}
The pointwise consequence $S(x)\le3\nu(x)+2$ shows why high elementary
representatives return immediately to a bounded region. The set
$\mathcal I_N$ and finite union $Y_N$ need not contain one another:
$41\in Y_{32}$ but $\nu(41)>32$. Their common closure is $\mathcal E_N$.
The new compiler accepts any finite bridge with target normalizing into
$Q_N$. At $N=384$, root415 of weight2 has normalized return1579,
which is above $N$ but equals $S^9(327)$. A stored48-row current certifies
both units using this known history. These are conditional constructions,
not a universal target-entry theorem.
In this example415 itself equals $S^4(327)$, so it demonstrates reuse of
supplied history rather than a new merger mechanism.
For any finite canonical $Q$ with $M=\max Q$, the same exact telescope
as in Theorem~\ref{thm:knownfibres} gives
\[
\left|\#(\nu^{-1}(Q)\cap[X,2X))-|Q|\right|
\le\lfloor\log_2 M\rfloor+1\qquad(X\in\mathbb Z,\ X\ge M).
\]
This additive bound alone gives no relative asymptotic for sparse $Q$.
Details and the finite compiler are in
\path{experiments/residual_progress/known_saturation/KNOWN_SATURATION.md}.

\section{A shrinking paired recursion beyond growing normalized returns}
The unbounded-return family in Theorem~\ref{thm:runbridge} supplies a
specific comparison path. For $a\ge7$, $a\equiv7\pmod{16}$, put
\[
x=2^a-1,\quad N=12\cdot2^{a-4},\quad q=2^{a-1}-1<N,
\quad w=\frac{3^{a-1}-1}{8}.
\]
Here $x$ is a complementary coefficient1 root at a required-ray cutoff.
Its normalized return is $3w\equiv1\pmod8$, with $3w/x$ unbounded,
while prescribed prefixes give
\[
S^{a-1}(q)=w,\qquad S^a(x)=4(3w)+1.
\]
Thus $w$ is certified by the supplied smaller seed $q$. It remains useful
to compare $3w$ with $w$, even when both lie far above $N$.

\begin{theorem}[A partial shrinking pair construction]\label{thm:pairedreturn}
Let $w>0$, $w\equiv3\pmod8$, and suppose
$h=v_2(3w-1)=2k+2$ is even, $k\ge1$. Set
\[
z=3^k\frac{3w-1}{2^h}.
\]
If $z$ is canonical, there is an explicit finite current with divergence
$\delta_{3w}-\delta_w$. If $z\equiv101\pmod{128}$, put
\[
w'=\frac{3z+1}{16}.
\]
Then $w'\equiv3\pmod8$, $0<w'<w/4$, and a finite current for
$\delta_{3w'}-\delta_{w'}$ explicitly supplies one for
$\delta_{3w}-\delta_w$. Repeated recognition terminates, returning
either a certificate or an unresolved arithmetic branch.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The identity $S(1+2^e c)=1+3\cdot2^{e-2}c$ for odd $c$ and
$e\ge3$ gives the exact paired prefixes
\[
S^k(3w)=4z+1,\qquad S^{k+1}(w)=2z+1.
\]
For canonical $z$, the two endpoints have elementary minimum $z$,
so appending their finite elementary bridges and subtracting the
prefixes gives the desired current. More precisely, if
$A=v_2(2z+2)$, their merger clock is
$S^{k+A}(3w)=S^{k+A+1}(w)$.

In the recursive case write $z=4u+1$, $u\equiv25\pmod{32}$.
Then $S(u)=w'$ and direct arithmetic gives
\[
S^{k+1}(3w)=w',\qquad S^{k+4}(w)=S(3w').
\]
The left prefix minus the right prefix, plus the current row at $3w'$,
has divergence
\[
\delta_{3w}-\delta_w+\delta_{3w'}-\delta_{w'}.
\]
The child current must therefore be subtracted. Finally
$z<(3/4)^{k+1}w<w$ and $(3z+1)/16<w/4$, giving the strict
integer decrease. Odd $h$ and other noncanonical $z$ are explicitly
unresolved; the theorem does not assert that every branch succeeds.
If a child merger has clock $l$, the parent has clock $k+l+2$.
\end{proof}

There are complete infinite families of every recursive depth. With
$k=1$, the recursive map is $R(w)=(27w+7)/256$. For $r\ge0$, the
unique binary cylinder
\[
27^r(229w-7)\equiv256^r(229\cdot59-7)
\pmod{2^{8r+7}}
\]
has exactly $r$ recursive transitions and then the terminal class
$w\equiv59\pmod{128}$. This follows from
$229R^r(w)-7=(27/256)^r(229w-7)$; the congruence enforces integrality
at every step. A child $v\equiv3\pmod8$ gives
$z=(16v-1)/3\equiv101\pmod{128}$ and exact $h=4$.
Consequently
\[
S^{3r+3}(3w)=S^{3r+4}(w).
\]
Elementary binary lifting of $3^{a-1}=1+8w$ produces a unique exponent
class $a=a_r\pmod{2^{8r+8}}$, with $a\equiv7\pmod{16}$,
for this cylinder. The lifting uses
$3^{2^j}\equiv1+2^{j+2}\pmod{2^{j+3}}$ for $j\ge1$.
The first classes are $a\equiv199\pmod{256}$ and
$a\equiv2887\pmod{65536}$. For every exponent in the relevant class,
\[
S^{a+3r+3}(2^a-1)=S^{a+3r+3}(2^{a-1}-1).
\]
These are actual smaller-seed mergers through the old unbounded returns.
The variable-$k$ branch also supplies $a\equiv39\pmod{2^{20}}$,
with one recursive $k=3$ step, a terminal $k=1$ step, and clock $a+8$.

Let $D_x$ be the old bridge from $x$ to $3w$, $J_w$ the accepted pair
current, and $H_{q,w}$ the prescribed smaller-seed prefix. Then
\[
\operatorname{div}(D_x+J_w-H_{q,w})=\delta_x-\delta_q.
\]
Adding a supplied exact current for $q$ certifies $x$, with all signed
rows retained. A nonnegative current is extracted using only this finite
graph, and any source weight is preserved by scaling the whole identity.
The stored example at $a=39$ gives a191-row current for
$x=549755813887$. The full proof, cylinders and checker are in
\path{experiments/residual_progress/second_return/SHRINKING_PAIRS.md}.

\section{Arbitrarily long growth with bounded run labels}
The normalized return of Theorem~\ref{thm:runbridge} is exactly
$T^{k(x)}(x)$ with $k(x)=a+\lfloor d/2\rfloor-1$.
For even $d$, $S^{k(x)}x=f(x)$; for odd $d$ it is $F(f(x))$.
Normalization and the semiconjugacy prove the statement in both cases.
Thus the formula supplies an exact positive variable clock, whose
iteration still requires a progress argument.

\begin{theorem}[Repeated bounded-run growth]\label{thm:boundedruns}
For every $r\ge1$, every positive seed satisfying
\[
x\equiv-9221\cdot4513^{-1}\pmod{2^{11r+6}}
\]
has $r$ repetitions of the ordinary valuation word
$(1,2,1,1,1,1,2,2)$. All these states are canonical, and its $2r$
normalized run returns alternate labels $(2,2),(5,4)$ and strictly
increase. Every noninitial ordinary prefix exceeds $x$.
For each $r$ there are such coefficient1 roots on infinitely many
required-ray cutoffs.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At27 the word gives
$27,41,31,47,71,107,161,121,91$. These canonical phases and labels
persist modulo $2^{17}$ by the exact affine prefix formulas.
Their slopes all exceed1. The paired return is
\[
\Psi(x)=\frac{6561x+9221}{2048},\quad
4513\Psi(x)+9221=\frac{6561}{2048}(4513x+9221),\quad
4513\cdot27+9221=2^{17}.
\]
The stated initial precision loses eleven bits per pair, leaving at
least17 bits before each of the first $r$ pairs. Each pair is therefore
integral, has the required phases and increases both return heights.
Every intermediate slope exceeds1 and has positive offset, so the
ordinary prefix also stays above the pair's starting value.

For the required rays, impose $x\equiv0\pmod3$ as well by CRT,
giving period $P=3\cdot2^{11r+6}$. At any $N=12\cdot2^b$ with
$b\ge11r+6$, its first representative above $N$ lies in
$(N,N+P]\subseteq(N,5N/4]$ and is $3\pmod{12}$.
It is a complementary coefficient1 root, and every displayed return
remains canonical and above $N$.
\end{proof}
The seed changes with $r$; the negative rational affine fixed point
$-9221/4513$ is not a positive divergent integer orbit.

There can be no potential $G(\ell(x),x)$ with a fixed finite label set,
nondecreasing in height for each label, that strictly decreases at every
normalized run return. A sufficiently long growing sequence repeats a
label at a larger height, contradicting strict decrease. This excludes
finite residue/height candidates of that form while allowing unbounded
state, nonmonotone potentials, intermediate increases and additional
mergers into supplied paths. The full prefix table and finite checks are in
\path{experiments/residual_progress/bounded_run_cycles/BOUNDED_RUN_GROWTH.md}.

\section{Merger scope and cancellation before high excursions}
The elementary successor identity proves, for every odd $x$,
\[
S(x)\in\{S(\nu(x)),3\nu(x)+2\}.
\]
Thus if $\nu(S^j x)\le N$ for some $j\le L$, an ordinary successor
at most $3N+2$ occurs within $L+1$ steps. For the saturated set
$\mathcal E_N$, the bound instead involves $\max Q_N$; no uniform
constant multiple of $N$ has been established as a bound for that maximum.
For the precisely defined symbolic recognizer testing normalized slopes
below $2/3$, with all needed binary bits charged, both rate factors of
the preceding prefix theorem persist for every fixed lookahead.
The escape cylinder $x\equiv191\pmod{256}$ has valuation word
$(1,1,1,1,1,2)$, canonical initial states, and final slope $729/128>3$.
Appending positive-barrier compositions and a final valuation1 gives
the matching lower rates via the bound
$\lambda_{j+1}\le3\sigma_j$ between ordinary and normalized slopes.
The bit-clock proof also retains the undetermined final normalization
branch. This result concerns the specified slope test, not arbitrary
bridges or integer counts with bit depth growing with $N$.

High future peaks need not be cancelled while high. For every
$a\ge19$, $a\equiv1\pmod{18}$, define
\[
N=12\cdot2^{a-5},\quad z=2^a-1,\quad
p=\frac{4z-1}{3},\quad q=4p+1.
\]
The annular table gives coefficient $+1$ at $p$ and $-1$ at $q$.
Since $S(p)=S(q)=z<p<q$, the current $\delta_p-\delta_q$ has
that same divergence and cancels these source units immediately.
Their shared future has $a-1$ valuation1 arrows and reaches
$2\cdot3^{a-1}-1$, whose ratio to either source is unbounded.
This identity assumes no future absorption.

Finally, allowing arbitrary common-tail comparisons with known absorbing
seeds gives exactly the basin of1: a common future with an absorbed seed
absorbs, and every absorbed seed meets the known seed1. This equivalence
provides no universal method to find a finite witness. It also means that
finite target-density samples cannot rule out every merger construction.

\section{What this changes, and what remains open}
The handoff's left-aligned remainder was paid for by a time horizon. That
created a source/time-domain issue and motivated a uniform positive current
cost target. The revised depth-one grouping places the remainder inside a
verified finite core. Theorems 1--3 consequently remove both requirements
from the logical route to Collatz.

The remaining target is precise: construct a finite current for $\PiR_N$,
possibly with an everywhere nonpositive residual, along the five rays in
Theorem \ref{thm:rays}. Alternatively, handle their annular increments in
Theorem \ref{thm:annular}; the 30 finite base cases have been discharged.
A proof of termination of that construction is essential.
Theorem \ref{thm:partial} now supplies actual infinite families of
partial certificates, and Theorem \ref{thm:norm} supplies a separate
one-pass contraction. Their residuals still need a closed construction.
Theorem \ref{thm:finitecompiler} converts a finite collection of supplied
packet certificates into ordinary absorption paths, but leaves the
universal supply of those certificates open.
Theorem \ref{thm:localinput} makes their local dependency explicit.
Theorem \ref{thm:unboundeddepth} rules out every fixed exposure depth,
even on one base-64 ladder. Theorem \ref{thm:auxnormal} supplies a
terminating partial rewrite, but its positive remainder cannot be
finished by more nonpositive current alone.
Its running time, number of rows, and positive cost may be arbitrarily large
functions of $N$. A small unresolved \emph{positive} residual is insufficient
for this all-horizon criterion unless its own future contribution is bounded.
Theorem~\ref{thm:release} now crosses selected arbitrarily long
isolations with exact nonpositive current, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:retained} improves automatic inputs by using their
retained graph. Theorem~\ref{thm:pathcollapse} preserves intermediate
source capacity and exposes the needed frontier information.
None covers every remaining component or proves a closed terminating
recursion.
Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedh} further rules out any exposure bound depending
only on the initial ternary order. Theorem~\ref{thm:halving} removes
half the leading outer residual with explicit smaller-seed bridges.
Corollary~\ref{cor:residualrays} gives the exact conditional induction
for its complementary family. Completing that family is the current
constructive target. Theorem~\ref{thm:runbridge} further reduces it but
leaves an explicit positive residual. Theorem~\ref{thm:bridgenormal}
supplies a closed quotient and all inverse branches, while
Theorem~\ref{thm:knownfibres} expands the known target set to arbitrary
heights. Neither target density nor the symbolic prefix rates establish
that every remaining branch reaches it.
Theorem~\ref{thm:saturation} now compiles all supplied history into an
exact finite core. Theorem~\ref{thm:pairedreturn} provides a genuinely
decreasing recursion for explicit infinite subfamilies of the growing
returns. Its odd-$h$ and remaining $z$ branches are the next concrete
obligation. Theorem~\ref{thm:boundedruns} rules out strict decrease on
every run return for height-monotone potentials with finitely many labels;
it leaves other arithmetic state and paired mergers available.
The packet work itself need not be nonpositive: direct finite iteration gives
$H_{39}(\PiR_{32})=57$. The target is a finite upper bound for each cutoff.

This is an equivalence with Collatz, so it does not establish that the
remaining construction is easy. The archive's currents refer to different
packet groupings and named finite cutoffs; they cannot be substituted for a
universal construction of $\PiR_N$.
