# C009 literature and novelty scope

The affine block identities and rational-cycle formula are established
background. Halbeisen–Hungerbühler's primary paper,
[Optimal bounds for the length of rational Collatz cycles](https://people.math.ethz.ch/~halorenz/publications/pdf/collatz.pdf),
was inspected again at Lemmas1–2 and equation(4): parity legality of
rational pseudo-cycles, the affine formula, and concatenation. It uses
the shortcut clock; C009 uses positive odd-only valuation blocks.
The endpoint reconstruction argument here is elementary, and the block
Smith calculation extends C004's recorded matrix argument. No novelty
is claimed for either.

The primary abstract of Alexey Teplinsky's
[Rotational Interval Exchange Transformations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.11211)
identifies first-return maps of circle rotations to finite unions of
arcs with a class of interval exchanges. This is relevant background:
return maps need not remain single rotations. Only the abstract was
used in this comparison; no theorem from its full text is imported.
C009 proves its finite one-point-deletion formula directly. It does
not transfer an irrational-rotation or interval-exchange theorem to
integer Collatz cycles, and adopting that terminology would not supply
the missing integer obstruction.

C009 improves no numerical cycle record and excludes no new
denominator-one word class. The Simons–de Weger and Hercher comparisons
and clock distinctions in [C001's primary audit](../../results/repeated-block-cancellation-bound/LITERATURE.md)
remain the relevant historical record. Neither the number of returns
to one dyadic band nor the number of deleted normalized ranks is
automatically the shortcut local-minimum count used in those papers.

The contribution of this batch is a checked failure of the proposed
compression mechanism, with actual rational-cycle controls and an
all-period finite-permutation explanation. It is not a new theorem
excluding Collatz cycles or a claim of priority in the literature.
