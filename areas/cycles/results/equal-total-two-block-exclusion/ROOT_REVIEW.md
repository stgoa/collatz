multiplicatively independent. At 2 and 3 the local negative terms
vanish; the remaining finite terms give minus the integer gcd logarithm.
The archimedean term can be negative, which strengthens the needed bound.

even if one repetition count stays bounded.

a height inequality. For f=X/Y-1 on (2^A,3^M), only the archimedean
negative term remains. A translated torus with both exponents nonzero
fixes both A and M. A fixed M has finitely many bad points as A grows;
a fixed A has finitely many positive pairs. These exhaust the exceptional
translate types, giving the uniform relative exponential bound for D>0.
