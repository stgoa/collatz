# Odd-order residue groups force full divisibility of signed return endpoints

## Statement

Let a nonempty sequence of exact signed odd-only arrows satisfy

```
2^a_i*x_(i+1)=3*x_i+d_i,   a_i>=1,   0<=i<m,
x_0=-h,   x_m=h,
```

where all states are nonzero odd integers, all addends `d_i` are
positive odd integers, and `h` is positive and odd. The addend may
vary between arrows; a constant addend is the signed `3x+d` map.
There is no absorption and no bound on the length, valuations,
addends or states.

**Endpoint divisibility.** Suppose an odd integer `Q>1` divides
every addend and there is an odd integer `R>=1` such that

```
2^R = 3^R = 1 (mod Q).                              (1)
```

Then `Q | h`. In particular, if a prime `p>3` has both
`ord_p(2)` and `ord_p(3)` odd, then

```
v_p(h) >= min_i v_p(d_i).                           (2)
```

Here `ord_p(b)` is the multiplicative order of `b` modulo `p`.
Condition (1) says that the residue group generated by 2 and 3
has odd order. The proof below uses (1) directly.

**Mersenne normal forms.** Consider either order of the two nonempty
segments in the [general normal form](../two-half-defect-mersenne-normal-form/README.md),
with addends `2^J-1` and `2^r-1`, `J>r>=1`, jointly joining `-h` to `h`.
Every prime in (2) satisfies

```
v_p(h) >= v_p(2^gcd(J,r)-1).                        (3)
```

Thus all the corresponding prime-power components of the common
addend must divide the signed endpoint. In particular,
`ord_p(2) | gcd(J,r)` forces `p | h`. This applies to both
orders and every interface sign, with the original strict
boundary-valuation conditions retained.

For the **endpoint-one, low-to-high subclass `h=r=1`**, the
first segment is a nonempty repetition of `-1 -> -1` under
`3x+1`. The second segment must therefore run from `-1` to `1`
under `3x+(2^J-1)`, with final valuation greater than `J`.
No such segment, and hence no such normal-form join, exists if

```
11 | J   or   25 | J.                              (4)
```

The obstruction in (4) does not need the last-valuation bound.
It excludes entire infinite level families; it is not a finite
orbit census. It does not exclude other endpoint values at those
levels, or settle the general half-defect integer-cycle question.

## Proof

### 1. Odd common exponents exclude a sum of powers

Put `g=gcd(Q,2^S+3^m)` for any nonnegative integers `S,m`.
Modulo `g`, we have `2^S=-3^m`. Raising to the odd exponent
`R` from (1) gives

```
1=(2^R)^S=-(3^R)^m=-1 (mod g).
```

Hence `g | 2`. Since `Q` is odd, `g` is odd, so `g=1`:

```
gcd(Q,2^S+3^m)=1.                                  (5)
```

Let `S=sum_i a_i`. Iterating the arrow equations modulo `Q`
eliminates every additive term and gives

```
2^S*x_m = 3^m*x_0 (mod Q).
```

With endpoints `-h,h`, this is `Q | h*(2^S+3^m)`.
Equation (5) therefore implies `Q | h`. This proof uses
only the exact integer arrow equations. It does not require
positivity of the intermediate states or a bound on their size.

### 2. Retaining every prime-power factor

Suppose the orders of 2 and 3 modulo an odd prime `p>3` are
odd. Their least common multiple gives an odd `R` satisfying
(1) modulo `p`. If `b^R=1 (mod p)`, then for each `e>=1`,

```
b^(R*p^(e-1)) = 1 (mod p^e).                       (6)
```

Indeed, if `b^L=1+p^k u`, its `p`th power is 1 modulo
`p^(k+1)` by the binomial expansion. Induction proves (6).
The lifted exponent remains odd. Apply part 1 with
`Q=p^e` and `e=min_i v_p(d_i)` when `e>0`; the case `e=0`
is automatic. This proves (2). Products of these prime powers
also divide `h`, since the components are coprime.

### 3. The two applications to Mersenne segments

For the two addends, the elementary Euclidean identity

```
gcd(2^J-1,2^r-1)=2^gcd(J,r)-1
```

turns (2) into (3). It follows by repeatedly replacing
`2^J-1` by its remainder `2^(J-r)-1` modulo `2^r-1`.

For `h=r=1` in low-to-high order, the first addend is 1.
Its exact arrow from `-1` has numerator `-2`, valuation 1,
and output `-1`; induction fixes the whole first segment.
Apply part 1 to the second segment alone. The certificate
checks primality and the exact orders in the following table:

| `p` | `ord_p(2)` | `ord_p(3)` | Odd common exponent `R` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 23 | 11 | 11 | 11 |
| 601 | 25 | 75 | 75 |

If 11 or 25 divides `J`, the respective prime divides
`2^J-1`, so part 1 would force that prime to divide `h=1`,
a contradiction. This proves (4). More generally, any prime
with the stated odd orders excludes this endpoint-one subclass
at every multiple of `ord_p(2)`.

## Remarks

- **The residual target.** Even `J` is already impossible for
  an endpoint-one high segment: `3 | 2^J-1`, so every output is
  divisible by 3. For odd `J`, every prime divisor `p` of
  `2^J-1` has odd `ord_p(2)`, since that order divides `J`.
  A return therefore requires `ord_p(3)` to be even for every
  such prime. At levels passing this necessary condition, the
  return problem and its high final valuation remain open here.
  No claim that every such level has a return is made.
- **Relation to earlier residues.** The
  [ordered-cycle residue result](../ordered-cycle-sorted-rank-residues/README.md)
  restricts a positive cycle's relation lattice under coprime
  total and ordering hypotheses; it explicitly allows prime
  denominators. The present assertion instead separates opposite
  endpoints and retains full prime powers of common additive
  parameters. Its normal-form application does not assume that
  the original cycle is ordered.
- **Prior work and novelty.** Multiplicative residue classes are
  elementary and already occur in generalized Collatz work.
  Wegner's [2021 paper, section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.08060v1)
  discusses the exponent lattice for positive cycles. A
  [public discussion from December 2024](https://www.reddit.com/r/Collatz/comments/1hly9jp/)
  explicitly identifies the two invariant residue classes of
  `3x+23`. This node gives a self-contained endpoint-divisibility
  proof and applies it to the repository's signed normal form;
  it makes no novelty claim for that residue observation.
  Novelty of the combined application is undetermined. Neither
  external reference is used as a proof input.
