# Integrality of all rotated cycle states is a single divisibility condition

## Statement

Let `w = (a_0, …, a_(m−1))`, `m ≥ 1`, all `a_i ≥ 1`, `D = 2^A − 3^m > 0`, and let `C_i` be the intercept of the word rotated to begin at `a_i`. Every `C_i` is positive and odd, `D` is odd and coprime to 3, and

```
2^(a_i)·C_(i+1) − 3·C_i = D,                       (1)
g = gcd(C_0, D) = gcd(C_i, D),   d_0 = D/g.         (2)
```

Let `B_w` be the cyclic `m × m` integer matrix with `−3` at `(i, i)` and `2^(a_i)` at `(i, i+1 mod m)` (entries added when `m = 1`), so (1) reads `B_w·C = D·1` and `det(B_w) = (−1)^(m−1)·D`.

1. **Smith theorem.** `B_w` has Smith form `diag(1, …, 1, D)`; `[B_w | −1]` has Smith form `diag(1, …, 1, g)`; its integer kernel is generated by `(C_0/g, …, C_(m−1)/g, D/g)`. Equivalently, `B_w·n = d·1` has an integer solution iff `d_0 | d`, all solutions are `t` times that vector, positive solutions need `t > 0`, and exact raw odd-only valuations need `t` odd. Also `gcd(C_0, …, C_(m−1)) = g`.
2. **One-letter cuts.** For `m ≥ 2`, cut a rotation into `u = (a_i)` and the remaining word `v`, with commutator `K_i = D_u·C_v − D_v·C_u`. Then

```
2^(a_i)·K_i = (2^(a_i) − 3)·C_i − D,                 (6)
gcd(D, K_i) = g·gcd(d_0, 2^(a_i) − 3).               (7)
```

At `a_i = 1` or `2` this is exactly `g`.
3. **Letter 1.** Every nontrivial positive integer `d = 1` raw cycle has a valuation-1 arrow: if all valuations were at least 2 and `M > 1` were its largest state, every incoming arrow would be at most `(3M+1)/4 < M`. The only remaining positive integer `d = 1` cycle is the fixed point 1.

Consequently a hypothetical nontrivial Collatz cycle would already have an exact single-cut gcd, and intersecting the bare all-rotation or all-cut gcd divisibilities cannot add a new cancellation filter.

## Proof

Written proof: [`ROTATION_LATTICE.md`](narrow-cycles-are-mechanical/ROTATION_LATTICE.md) §§1–2. For a prime `p | D`, the recurrence mod `p` determines a kernel vector from its first coordinate (each `2^(a_i)` is invertible), and the consistency condition `2^A ≡ 3^m (mod p)` already holds, so the kernel has dimension exactly one; with `det(B_w) = ±D` every Smith factor but the last is 1. By Cramer's rule the absolute maximal minors of `[B_w | −1]` are `D, C_0, …, C_(m−1)`, whose gcd is `g`. The kernel vector is primitive since `gcd(n_0, d_0) = 1`. Identity (6) is the concatenation identity (it is C001's commutator at `k = 1`), and (7) follows because `2^(a_i)` is a unit modulo `D` and `gcd(n_i, d_0) = 1`.

## Lean

Partly formal (Lean unit C004-a): `LeanLane.Cyc.cut_identity` and `LeanLane.Cyc.cut_gcd` are (6) and (7) for a one-letter cut, for `a_i ≥ 1`. The Smith forms of claim 1 were not formalised (Smith normal form is thin in Mathlib), nor was the letter-1 lemma. Status therefore stays `reviewed`.

## Remarks

- **What it says and does not say.** Making all rotated states integral supplies exactly the original denominator-divisibility condition, so these congruences cannot be treated as independent filters or independent random events. This concerns combining bare divisibilities; it says nothing against **size bounds** on cut commutators that exploit the particular word. Positive odd scaling is required for an actual raw odd-only cycle; the integer kernel also contains points outside that domain.
- **Controls.** For `(1,2,2)^2`, `D = 295`, `g = 59`, and the reduced denominator remains 5: the primitive-period clause and the `g` factor matter. Words `(2)`, `(1,2,2)`, `(1,3)`, `(4)` give `D = 1, 5, 7, 13` with reduced denominators `1, 5, 7, 13`.
