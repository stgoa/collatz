# C014 — Source scope and comparison

The partition, representation, rotation-resultant and explicit family
arguments in this batch are proved with elementary algebra and word
combinatorics. They import no new analytic estimate or formal theorem.
The existence of arbitrarily large primes is sufficient for the
prime-period coverage family. The mechanical prefix construction is
the one already used in the repository's R71/C004 work.

Gabrić's [Words that almost commute](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.01120v2)
was read again for its definitions and statements concerning words
and cyclic rotations. Rotation Hamming distance is an established
combinatorial object. The paper's two-position theory does not provide
the denominator estimate sought here. This batch proves its particular
partition and column statements directly; it makes no novelty claim
for those elementary consequences.

The whole-word polynomial here has coefficients given by powers of two
at valuation prefixes. Papers using the name “Collatz polynomials” for
polynomials whose coefficients are successive orbit values concern
different objects. Search results about those objects were not adopted
as results about P_w and Q. Forum posts and unverified repository claims
were not mathematical premises.

The exact resultant formula follows from an explicit polynomial
identity and the product over the roots of a binomial. The growth
comparison uses exact integer inequalities. The exponent of 3 removed
from the quotient is derived by factoring a geometric sum; no
unproved prime distribution or uniform gcd estimate is used.

The [C011 source audit](../C011/LITERATURE.md) remains the record for
Matveev and the Hercher/Barina inputs to its two-defect exclusion.
C014 neither improves a universal numerical cycle record nor supplies
a new all-size q1 exclusion. Its resultant obstruction already occurs
inside C011's excluded two-defect class, whereas its prime-period
coverage family has rotation distance three. These are controls on
proposed reductions, not claims that published cycle exclusions fail.

The [C013 common-background theorem](../C013/OVERLAP_CONTROLS.md)
remains valid. C014 explains why its representation hypothesis cannot
be supplied merely by changing cuts or repeating a prime-period word.
The general denominator candidate and growing rotation-distance target
remain open. Correctness, usefulness and novelty are separate; no
independent formal verification or novelty against the full literature
is claimed.
