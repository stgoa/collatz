# C013 — Sources and comparison

## Explicit analytic input

The effective bounded-denominator finiteness and the denominator-one
complexity restriction use the same published rational form of Matveev
as [C011](../C011/EXCLUSION.md). Languasco, Luca, Moree and Togbe,
[Sequences of integers generated by two fixed primes, Theorem 2.1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12188-025-00293-9),
state the estimate directly for a nonzero product of rational powers
minus one. With bases 2,3 and exponents -A,m, this is precisely
`log(1-3^m/2^A)>-600000000*(1+log(2m))` when `0<A<2m`
and `2^A>3^m`. All logarithms here are natural.

The source's displayed definition of Lambda and Theorem 2.1 were
rechecked during this batch. The side feed suggests a further log(2)
loss when converting a linear-form estimate to this expression. That
conversion is unnecessary for the theorem actually cited: its input
already is the product minus one. No correction to C011 is justified
by that remark. This assessment does not assert that every version of
Matveev has the same displayed form.

The analytic theorem is imported, not re-proved or formally verified.
C013's finiteness and complexity consequences do not use the published
Hercher/Barina period cutoff. C011's all-size two-defect exclusion still
has those separate dependencies; its [source audit](../C011/LITERATURE.md)
retains their clocks, endpoints and computational provenance.

## Elementary inputs and attribution

The polynomial identities, homogeneous gcd extraction, resultant bound
and finite Fourier support argument are proved in
[OVERLAP_CONTROLS](OVERLAP_CONTROLS.md). In particular, the support-product
inequality follows there from Cauchy--Schwarz and Parseval; it is not
presented as a new uncertainty principle. Classical background is
Donoho and Stark, [Uncertainty principles and signal recovery](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/abs/10.1137/0149053),
SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics 49 (1989), 906--931;
an [author-hosted copy](https://web.stanford.edu/dept/statistics/cgi-bin/donoho/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/UPSR.pdf)
is available. No additional theorem from that paper is a premise here.

The paired-run divisibility and the near-critical family in
[OVERLAP_THEORY](OVERLAP_THEORY.md) are derived directly from exact
affine compositions. The existence of upper continued-fraction
convergents to the irrational number log(3)/log(2), with error less
than the reciprocal square of the denominator, is the standard
approximation input. The rescaling that keeps the product of length
and logarithmic drift between 1 and 4 is proved in that note.

## What this adds and what it does not

C012 showed why ignoring correlations among shared arcs loses essential
information. C013 removes exact polynomial common factors for one
arbitrary background repeated between any nonconstant marker pattern.
The residual degree obeys an all-rotation Fourier bound. Charging both
the resultant and the remaining background intercept gives a uniform
denominator estimate and effective finiteness at fixed marker count
and total. Both the markers and the entire background may vary within
those bounds.

This differs from holding each shared-arc intercept fixed. It also
leaves a serious limitation: arbitrary sparse rotations need not have
one identical background. The marker count r is not the rotation
mismatch count s. The necessary q1 restriction is therefore a theorem
about the declared representations, not the desired unrestricted
rotation-distance theorem.

The actual q5 period-17 cycle has no polynomial common factor but has
numerical cancellation 1,015,513. Thus polynomial coprimality alone
does not settle the arithmetic at evaluation. The primitive six-marker
example conversely shows that polynomial cancellation is not confined
to repeated marker words. Both controls are necessary.

No universal numerical cycle record is improved. No all-size q1
exclusion for every nonconstant marker pattern follows from the
finiteness assertion; finite exceptions remain. Novelty against the
full published cycle literature is unassessed. Abstract-level leads
and search results about other Collatz polynomials were not imported
as theorems about these particular polynomials.
