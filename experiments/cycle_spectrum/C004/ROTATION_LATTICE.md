# C004 — Rotation lattice and narrow-cycle rigidity

**Status:** written elementary proofs and
[independent review](INDEPENDENT_REVIEW.md) completed. This is a
restriction on positive periodic odd-only orbits, not a proof of Collatz
convergence, a novelty claim, or an argument about divergent orbits. All
arrows here use the raw map, including at state 1. In the usual absorbing
convention, the cycle at 1 is the trivial terminal cycle.

## 1. Objects and exact cycle equations

Let `w=(a_0,...,a_(m-1))`, with `m>=1` and every `a_i>=1`. Put

```
A_i = a_0+...+a_(i-1),  A=A_m,
C_0 = sum_(j=0)^(m-1) 3^(m-1-j) 2^A_j,
D = 2^A-3^m > 0.
```

The raw odd-only map is `S_d(x)=(3x+d)/2^v2(3x+d)` on positive odd
integers, with no absorption at 1. Write `C_i` for the intercept of the
word rotated to begin at `a_i`. Every `C_i` is positive and odd, while `D`
is positive, odd, and coprime to 3. The affine fixed point and its rotations
are the positive rational states `x_i=C_i/D`; their valuations are exactly
the displayed `a_i`, since

```
2^a_i C_(i+1) - 3 C_i = D.                         (1)
```

This also shows that all rotations have one reduced denominator

```
g = gcd(C_0,D) = gcd(C_i,D),   d_0=D/g,            (2)
```

because `C_(i+1) = 3*2^(-a_i)*C_i (mod D)` and 2,3 are units modulo `D`.
The normalized primitive integer numerator at phase `i` is `n_i=C_i/g`.
It is odd, coprime to `d_0`, and `S_(d_0)(n_i)=n_(i+1)` exactly.

## 2. The complete integer lattice of all rotations

Let `B_w` be the cyclic `m` by `m` integer matrix whose row `i` has `-3`
at column `i` and `2^a_i` at column `i+1 mod m`, adding the two entries
when `m=1`. Thus `(1)` is `B_w C=D 1`, and direct expansion gives

```
det(B_w)=(-1)^(m-1) D.                             (3)
```

**Smith theorem.** The Smith form of `B_w` is
`diag(1,...,1,D)`. The Smith form of the full-rank augmented matrix
`[B_w | -1]` is `diag(1,...,1,g)`. Its integer kernel is generated by

```
(n_0,...,n_(m-1),d_0) = (C_0/g,...,C_(m-1)/g,D/g).  (4)
```

**Proof.** For a prime `p|D`, the recurrence modulo `p` determines every
coordinate of a vector in `ker(B_w mod p)` from its first coordinate,
because each `2^a_i` is invertible. The consistency condition around the
cycle is `2^A=3^m (mod p)`, already true. Hence this kernel has dimension
exactly one. At primes not dividing `D`, the matrix is invertible. Together
with `(3)`, this proves that every Smith factor except the last is 1.
This includes `m=1`.

By Cramer's rule, the absolute maximal minors of `[B_w | -1]` are
`D,C_0,...,C_(m-1)`. Their gcd is `g` by `(2)`. Its first `m-1` Smith
factors are 1 because those of the submatrix `B_w` already are. This
proves its stated Smith form. The rational kernel is one dimensional,
and the integer vector in `(4)` is primitive since its coordinate gcd is
`gcd(n_0,d_0)=1`; it therefore generates the entire integer kernel. QED.

Equivalently, the class of `1` in `coker(B_w)` has exact order `d_0`.
An integer solution to `B_w n=d 1` exists **if and only if** `d` is an
integer multiple of `d_0`. All integer solutions are `t` times `(4)`.
Positive solutions require `t>0`; exact raw odd-only valuations require
`t` odd. If the supported parameter domain additionally requires
`gcd(d,6)=1`, then `gcd(t,6)=1` is also required, since `d_0` is coprime
to 6. Even `t` gives even coordinates and does not realize this raw
valuation word.

Thus simultaneous divisibility of **all** rotated states is precisely one
integer-lattice condition. In particular,

```
gcd(C_0,C_1,...,C_(m-1)) = g:                    (5)
```

`g` divides every coordinate by `(2)`, and any common divisor of all
coordinates divides `D` by `(1)`.

### One-letter cuts and an exact obstruction to independent gcd filters

For `m>=2`, cut a rotation into the first letter `u=(a_i)` and the
remaining word `v`. Set `D_u=2^a_i-3`, `D_v=2^sum(v)-3^(m-1)`, with
intercepts `C_u=1,C_v`, and commutator `K_i=D_u C_v-D_v C_u`. The
concatenation identity yields

```
2^a_i K_i = (2^a_i-3) C_i-D,                   (6)
gcd(D,K_i) = g*gcd(d_0,2^a_i-3).                (7)
```

Indeed `2^a_i` is a unit modulo `D` and `gcd(n_i,d_0)=1`.
Each such cut bound retains `g`; at a letter 1 or 2,
`2^a_i-3=-1` or `1`, so its gcd is **exactly** `g`. Every nontrivial
positive integer **d=1** raw cycle has a letter 1: if all valuations
were at least 2 and `M>1` were its largest state, every incoming arrow
would be at most `(3M+1)/4<M`. The only remaining positive integer
d=1 cycle is the fixed point 1 with valuation 2. Consequently a
hypothetical nontrivial Collatz cycle would already have an exact
single-cut gcd. Intersecting
the bare all-rotation or all-cut gcd divisibilities cannot add a new
cancellation filter. This says nothing against **size bounds** on cut
commutators that exploit the particular word.

## 3. Exact narrow-annulus classification

Here `m` denotes the **primitive state period**, so its positive rational
states are distinct. Let `L=min_i x_i` and `H=max_i x_i`.

**Narrow-cycle theorem.** If `m>1`, then

```
H < 2L  if and only if  w is a cyclic rotation of
a_j = floor(((j+1)A)/m)-floor((jA)/m),             (8)
```

the mechanical valuation word with its actual total `A`. In this case
`gcd(A,m)=1`, and the reduced denominator is exactly `D`. A fixed point
(`m=1`) is mechanically coded and has `H=L`.

**Forward proof without a circle-map theorem.** Sort the distinct states
as `L=y_0<y_1<...<y_(m-1)=H`. Put `f_j=3y_j+1`, which is strictly
increasing. Narrowness gives `f_(m-1)<2f_0`. The unique powers of 2
that move each `f_j` into `[L,2L)` have exponents `q` or `q+1`, where
`q=floor(log_2(f_0/L))`. Since these powers are the exact valuations
of the cycle, the high exponent `q+1` occupies a final block of `h`
sorted indices. A primitive cycle with `m>1` has `1<=h<=m-1`: if
`h=0`, each successor preserves sorted rank and all states are fixed.

After division, the high block comes **before** the low block in the
sorted output. Indeed every high output is at most
`f_(m-1)/2^(q+1) < f_0/2^q`, while outputs within each block remain
increasing. The successor of sorted rank `j` therefore has rank
`j+h mod m`. Since the orbit visits all `m` states, `gcd(h,m)=1`.
Beginning at the minimum, time `i` has sorted rank `i h mod m`; it
receives valuation `q+1` exactly on a carry across `m`:

```
a_i = q + floor(((i+1)h)/m)-floor((i h)/m).
```

The total is `A=mq+h`, so this is `(8)`. Other starting phases are its
cyclic rotations.

**Reverse proof.** For a mechanical word with primitive totals
`gcd(A,m)=1`, let `C_l` be the intercept of phase `l`, `0<=l<m`, as in
R71. Every phase is a cyclic rotation, and in each summand

```
0 <= floor((jA+l)/m)-floor(jA/m) <= 1.
```

The `j=0` summand does not change. Hence `C_0<=C_l<2C_0` for every
phase, and division by the common positive `D` gives `H<2L`. If a
displayed word is a repetition, reduce to its primitive state period;
the repeated mechanical formula reduces to the same primitive formula.
This proves the equivalence. QED.

R71's [exact denominator theorem](../../residual_progress/lattice_cycles/BALANCED_DENOMINATORS.md)
gives `g=1` for a primitive mechanical word. Its elementary mod-8
argument shows `D=1` cannot occur at `m>1`; the only integer mechanical
cycle is the fixed point 1. Therefore every hypothetical nontrivial
positive **integer** odd-only Collatz cycle must satisfy

```
H > 2L.                                            (9)
```

Equality cannot occur: all reduced cycle numerators are odd and have
one common odd denominator, so one state cannot be twice another.
The theorem leaves every wide cycle and every aperiodic orbit open.
It is an intrinsic condition on arbitrary primitive words, unlike the
repeated-block hypotheses of C001–C003.

**Exact narrow-cycle spectrum.** Fix a positive odd parameter `d`, and
consider raw positive odd integer cycles. Up to cyclic rotation, every
primitive cycle with `H<2L` arises from exactly one coprime pair
`(A,m)` with `A>=m>=1`, `2^A>3^m`, and

```
D=2^A-3^m divides d.                              (10)
```

Its word is `(8)` and its states are `(d/D) C_l`, where `C_l` ranges
through the mechanical phases. Conversely each such pair satisfying
`(10)` gives a positive raw odd cycle of primitive period `m`:
R71 gives `g=1`, the phase words are distinct rotations when `m>1`,
and the scale `d/D` is odd. This includes `m=1`, where every fixed
cycle has valuation `A>=2`. For the supported domain `gcd(d,6)=1`,
the scaling factor is also coprime to 6. At `d=1`, only `(A,m)=(2,1)`
survives. The statement classifies this narrow subfamily; it does not
enumerate all solutions to `(10)` for an arbitrary fixed `d`.

## 4. Controls, a rejected size rule, and limits

| Raw word | `D` | Rotated intercepts | Reduced denominator | Size control |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | --- |
| `(2)` | 1 | `(1)` | 1 | Fixed point 1; absorber in the standard d=1 convention. |
| `(1,2,2)` | 5 | `(23,37,29)` | 5 | Mechanical; `37/23<2`, valid d=5 raw cycle. |
| `(1,3)` | 7 | `(5,11)` | 7 | Nonmechanical; `11/5>2`, valid d=7 raw cycle. |
| `(4)` | 13 | `(1)` | 13 | Fixed rational state `1/13`, valid d=13 raw state 1. |

For `(1,2,2)^2`, `D=295`, `g=59`, and the reduced denominator remains
5. This is why the primitive-period clause in Section 3 and the `g`
factor in Section 2 matter.

A tempting stronger rule, “some pair of distinct rotated states lies
less than 2 apart,” is false. The exact raw d=5 cycle with primitive
states `(19,31,49)` has minimum pair gap `12/5>2`. The
[finite rotation audit](rotation_audit.json) checks all rotations of
the pinned actual-cycle catalogue and preserves 28 distinct normalized
counterexamples to that rule. The same audit tests the narrow theorem
on a bounded generated grid. These checks are evidence for the stated
domain; the universal claims above rest on the written proofs.

A large cycle minimum is consistent with nonintegrality. R71's
mechanical upper-approximant family has minimum greater than `m/6`
for arbitrarily large `m` while retaining reduced denominator greater
than 1 (which can be smaller than the unreduced `D` for proper powers).
The narrow theorem says those cycles are mechanical, not that one of
their states is small.
